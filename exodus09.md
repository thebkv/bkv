# Exodus 9 — The Word of the LORD Creates a Division

## BKV Insight

Exodus 9 describes a deepening stage in the dismantling of **the ruling consciousness of bondage**. Judgment moves from the environment of Egypt into its possessions, bodies, agriculture, and accumulated strength, while God continues to preserve what belongs to Israel. Yet the most important development occurs within Egypt itself: some of Pharaoh's servants begin to **fear the word of the LORD** and act upon it, while others disregard it and suffer the consequence. The division is therefore becoming more precise: the disciple's inner world is no longer divided merely between Israel and Egypt, but increasingly between **that which hears and responds to God's word and that which remains hardened against it**.

## Canonical History & Context

Exodus 9 contains the fifth, sixth, and seventh plagues. Moses again commands Pharaoh to release Israel so that they may serve the LORD. When Pharaoh refuses, a severe pestilence strikes Egypt's livestock while Israel's livestock is preserved. Moses and Aaron then scatter ashes from a furnace before Pharaoh, producing boils upon the Egyptians and their animals; even the magicians can no longer stand before Moses. God next announces a devastating hailstorm and explains more fully why Pharaoh has been allowed to remain: God's power and name are being declared throughout the earth. For the first time, Egyptians who fear the word of the LORD bring their servants and cattle into shelter and are preserved, while those who disregard the warning leave them in the field. Hail and fire devastate Egypt, although Goshen remains untouched. Pharaoh confesses that he has sinned and that the LORD is righteous, but when the storm ends he hardens his heart again.

## Natural Reading

### v1–4 — The Division Reaches the Possessions

Then the LORD said unto **Moses — the Drawn-Out One**:

> “Go in unto Pharaoh, and tell him, Thus saith the LORD God of the Hebrews, Let my people go, that they may serve me.”

Again the demand remains unchanged:

**LET MY PEOPLE GO → THAT THEY MAY SERVE ME**

If Pharaoh continues to hold the faculties under his government, the judgment will now reach the **cattle, horses, asses, camels, oxen, and sheep** of Egypt.

The conflict is moving progressively deeper into the substance and productive strength of the old order.

And God says:

> “The LORD shall sever between the cattle of Israel and the cattle of Egypt.”

The division established in Exodus 8 continues.

God is not merely judging indiscriminately.

He is **distinguishing what belongs to His people from what belongs to the ruling order of bondage**.

### v5–7 — Egypt Loses Its Productive Strength

And the LORD appointed a set time.

And on the morrow:

> “all the cattle of Egypt died: but of the cattle of the children of Israel died not one.”

The productive strength of Egypt is struck.

Yet:

**“not one”**

of Israel's cattle dies.

Pharaoh sends to investigate and discovers that the distinction is real.

Still:

> “the heart of Pharaoh was hardened, and he did not let the people go.”

Evidence has now become unmistakable.

Egypt is being diminished.

Israel is being preserved.

But the ruling consciousness continues to resist what the evidence reveals.

### v8–10 — The Furnace Becomes the Source of Affliction

And the LORD said unto Moses and Aaron:

> “Take to you handfuls of ashes of the furnace.”

Moses scatters the ashes toward heaven before Pharaoh.

And the ashes become a fine dust throughout Egypt, producing boils upon man and beast.

The **furnace** is especially significant in the Exodus story.

Egypt is later remembered as:

> **“the iron furnace.”**

The place of forced production and affliction now supplies the material through which **Egypt itself is afflicted**.

What the system used as an instrument of bondage returns upon the system.

The furnace produces its own judgment.

### v11 — The Magicians Can No Longer Stand

And the magicians could not stand before Moses because of the boils.

Earlier they could imitate.

Then they failed to imitate and confessed:

> **“This is the finger of God.”**

Now:

> **they cannot even stand before Moses.**

The counterfeit powers of Egypt are progressively collapsing.

**Imitation → Failure → Inability to Stand**

What once appeared to rival divine authority can no longer maintain its position in the Presence of that authority.

### v12 — Pharaoh Remains Hardened

And the LORD hardened the heart of Pharaoh, and he hearkened not unto them.

The lesser powers of Egypt have already begun yielding to the evidence.

The central ruling will does not.

The resistance is concentrating around **Pharaoh himself**.

### v13–14 — The Conflict Reaches the Heart

And the LORD says:

> “Rise up early in the morning, and stand before Pharaoh.”

Again:

> “Let my people go, that they may serve me.”

Then God says:

> “For I will at this time send all my plagues upon **thine heart**.”

The confrontation is moving inward.

The plagues have touched:

**water,  
land,  
houses,  
bodies,  
possessions.**

Now God identifies the true center of the conflict:

**the heart of the ruling consciousness**.

The external judgments reveal an inward condition.

### v15–16 — Why Pharaoh Has Been Allowed to Stand

God tells Pharaoh that He could already have destroyed him and Egypt.

But:

> “in very deed for this cause have I raised thee up, for to shew in thee my power.”

And:

> “that my name may be declared throughout all the earth.”

Pharaoh's continued existence does not demonstrate Pharaoh's ultimate power.

Even his resistance is being incorporated into the revelation of **God's authority**.

The harder the ruling consciousness resists, the more completely its inability to prevail will eventually be revealed.

### v17 — Pharaoh Still Exalts Himself

God identifies the condition plainly:

> “As yet exaltest thou thyself against my people, that thou wilt not let them go?”

At the center of bondage is **self-exaltation**.

Pharaoh places his own governing will above God's claim upon Israel.

The issue is no longer hidden beneath economic or political language.

It is the elevation of one authority against another.

### v18–19 — A Warning Is Given Before Judgment

God announces:

> “to morrow about this time I will cause it to rain a very grievous hail.”

But then something new happens.

God gives Egypt a warning:

> “Send therefore now, and gather thy cattle, and all that thou hast in the field.”

Whatever is brought into shelter will live.

Whatever remains exposed will die.

The word of God now creates a **choice within Egypt itself**.

Judgment has been announced.

But the response to the word will determine what happens next.

### v20–21 — A New Division Appears

> **“He that feared the word of the LORD among the servants of Pharaoh made his servants and his cattle flee into the houses.”**

But:

> **“he that regarded not the word of the LORD left his servants and his cattle in the field.”**

This is a major development.

The division is no longer simply:

**ISRAEL / EGYPT**

Now there is a division **within Egypt**:

**those who fear the word of the LORD**

and

**those who disregard the word of the LORD**.

Some faculties still located within the old order have begun to recognize the higher authority.

And recognition produces **action**.

They hear.

They believe.

They move what is under their care into safety.

The Word itself has become the dividing line.

### v22–26 — Hail and Fire Fall Upon Egypt

And Moses stretches forth his rod toward heaven.

And the LORD sends:

**thunder,  
hail,  
and fire running along upon the ground.**

The judgment now descends from **heaven** upon the land of Egypt.

Everything exposed in the field is struck:

**man,  
beast,  
herb,  
tree.**

The structures that remain exposed beneath Pharaoh's government are broken.

But:

> “Only in the land of Goshen, where the children of Israel were, was there no hail.”

Again the distinction is maintained.

The same event falls upon the larger field, yet the condition under God is not the same as the condition under Pharaoh.

### v27–28 — Pharaoh Confesses

And Pharaoh sends for Moses and Aaron and says:

> **“I have sinned this time: the LORD is righteous, and I and my people are wicked.”**

This is Pharaoh's strongest confession so far.

The ruler who once asked:

> **“Who is the LORD?”**

now says:

> **“The LORD is righteous.”**

The revelation has progressed.

Pharaoh can no longer plausibly deny God's power.

He asks Moses:

> “Intreat the LORD... and I will let you go.”

The old ruling consciousness is now capable of **confession under pressure**.

The remaining question is whether confession has reached the heart.

### v29–30 — Moses Discerns Pharaoh's Condition

And Moses says:

> “As soon as I am gone out of the city, I will spread abroad my hands unto the LORD.”

The thunder will cease.

The hail will end.

Why?

> “that thou mayest know how that the earth is the LORD'S.”

Pharaoh claims government over Egypt.

God reveals a deeper ownership:

> **“The earth is the LORD'S.”**

But Moses adds:

> “I know that ye will not yet fear the LORD God.”

Moses can now distinguish between **words spoken under pressure** and actual inward reorientation.

Pharaoh has confessed.

But he has not yet truly **feared the LORD**.

### v31–32 — Some Growth Is Destroyed; Some Remains

And the flax and barley were smitten, because they were grown.

But the wheat and rye were not smitten:

> “for they were not grown up.”

Even within the judgment there are stages.

What has already emerged into the exposed field is struck.

What has not yet appeared remains for later.

The dismantling of Egypt is **progressive**.

Not everything falls at once.

### v33–35 — Relief Reveals the Heart Again

And Moses goes out from Pharaoh and spreads abroad his hands unto the LORD.

The thunder ceases.

The hail ends.

The rain is no longer poured upon the earth.

And when Pharaoh sees that the judgment has ceased:

> “he sinned yet more, and hardened his heart.”

Again:

**Pressure → Confession → Relief → Hardening**

Pharaoh's words changed under judgment.

His governing orientation did not.

And he would not let Israel go.

The chapter therefore ends with a much sharper distinction than it began.

Some within Egypt have heard the Word and acted upon it.

Pharaoh has heard the Word, witnessed its fulfillment, confessed God's righteousness, and still returned to resistance.

The issue is becoming unmistakable:

**hearing the Word is not enough.  
Acknowledging the Word is not enough.  
The decisive question is whether the governing will will actually yield to it.**

## Fractal Notes & References

Exodus 9 develops the biblical pattern of **the Word producing division according to response**. God warns before the hail, and Egyptians who “feared the word of the LORD” act upon it and preserve what has been entrusted to them. This anticipates the repeated scriptural distinction between merely hearing God's word and actually doing it: Noah believes the warning of things “not seen as yet” and prepares the ark (Hebrews 11:7); Jesus describes the wise man as the one who **hears His sayings and does them** (Matthew 7:24); and the Passover will soon make the same principle even more explicit when Israel must hear God's instruction and act upon it before judgment passes through Egypt. The chapter also advances the progressive collapse of counterfeit authority: the magicians first imitate God's signs, then fail to reproduce them, and now cannot even stand before Moses. Within the Exodus movement, **the Word of God itself is increasingly becoming the boundary by which the inner field is separated and reordered**.

## Onomastics & Name Meanings

| Name | Textual / Linguistic Meaning | BKV Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Moses — Mosheh** | Exodus 2:10 connects the name with *mashah*, **“to draw out”** | **The Drawn-Out One / delivering consciousness**, now acting with increasing confidence under divine authority |
| **Aaron — Aharon** | Etymology uncertain; Scripture does not self-define the name | Functionally **the expressive faculty joined to Moses**, participating in the manifestation of God's judgment |
| **Israel — Yisrael** | Genesis 32:28 associates the name with striving with God and prevailing | **God-Prevails**; the faculties increasingly distinguished and preserved under divine government |
| **Egypt — Mitsrayim** | Historical name of Egypt | **The field of bondage / foreign inner government**, now being dismantled at progressively deeper levels |
| **Pharaoh** | Egyptian royal title from **“great house”** | **The central ruling will of the old order**, increasingly isolated in its resistance to God |
| **Goshen — Goshen** | Probably Egyptian; exact etymology uncertain | Its textual function remains decisive: **the distinguished territory occupied by God's people within Egypt**, preserved while judgment falls around it |

Exodus 9 introduces an important phrase that is more useful than forcing additional name meanings:

> **“He that feared the word of the LORD...”**

That person is still described as one of:

> **“the servants of Pharaoh.”**

Yet something has changed internally.

He remains within Egypt.

He remains institutionally under Pharaoh.

But when God's word contradicts Pharaoh's world, **he acts according to God's word**.

That gives us an important refinement of the Exodus pattern:

**Physical departure comes later.  
Reorientation can begin before departure.**

The decisive separation starts when the Word of God becomes more authoritative within the disciple than the ruling assumptions of Egypt.
