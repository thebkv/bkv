# Exodus 27 — The Outer Life Is Ordered Toward the Presence of God

## BKV Insight

Exodus 27 shows how the life outside the inner sanctuary is brought into right relation with God. The bronze altar receives what is offered, the court establishes the boundary of sacred space, the gate gives an appointed way in, and pure oil keeps the lamp burning continually before the LORD. **The disciple’s outer life must therefore be ordered through surrender, boundary, and sustained illumination so that everything surrounding the inner dwelling serves the Presence at its center.**

## Canonical History & Context

Exodus 27 continues the Tabernacle instructions given to Moses on Sinai. Exodus 25 revealed the Ark, Mercy Seat, Table, and Lampstand, while Exodus 26 built the dwelling around them with curtains, boards, coverings, veil, and entrance. Exodus 27 now moves into the outer court, describing the bronze altar of burnt offering, the court and its hangings, the gate on the east side, and the pure olive oil required to keep the lamp burning continually. The chapter completes the outward structure of approach and prepares for Exodus 28, where Aaron and his sons are clothed and appointed to minister within this sacred order.

## Natural Reading

### v1–8 — The Altar Receives What Is Offered

> “And thou shalt make an altar of shittim wood.”

The first great object in the outer court is the altar.

It stands between the entrance and the dwelling, establishing **sacrifice as part of the approach toward God**.

The altar is square, with horns upon its four corners, and is overlaid with brass. Its pans, shovels, basins, fleshhooks, firepans, grate, rings, and staves are all made according to the pattern.

The fire is therefore contained within an ordered structure. What is brought to God is not thrown into uncontrolled destruction; it is **offered at the place appointed for surrender**.

The horns rise from the altar itself, joining strength to sacrifice.

The altar is also built to travel:

> “the staves shall be put into the rings, and the staves shall be upon the two sides of the altar, to bear it.”

Israel will carry the altar through the wilderness.

The need to bring the life into surrender before God travels with the people.

And again the LORD says:

> **“as it was shewed thee in the mount, so shall they make it.”**

Even sacrifice is governed by the divine pattern.

### v9–19 — The Court Establishes the Sacred Field

> “And thou shalt make the court of the tabernacle.”

Fine linen hangings, pillars, sockets, hooks, and fillets define a measured enclosure around the dwelling.

The sacred space now has a clear outer boundary.

The structure is:

**wilderness  
→ court  
→ dwelling  
→ Holy Place  
→ Most Holy Place**

The court gathers the outer life into an ordered field around the Presence.

Its measurements are deliberate. Its sides are established. Its pillars stand in appointed places.

The sacred life therefore has **form and boundary**.

The Tabernacle is not left open to the wilderness on every side. The life ordered toward God knows what belongs within the sacred field and where the point of entry lies.

Even the smaller structural pieces matter:

> “All the vessels of the tabernacle in all the service thereof, and all the pins thereof, and all the pins of the court, shall be of brass.”

The dwelling stands because great and small parts alike fulfill their appointed work.

### v13–16 — The Gate Gives an Appointed Way In

The east side of the court contains the gate.

> “And for the gate of the court shall be an hanging of twenty cubits, of blue, and purple, and scarlet, and fine twined linen.”

The same sacred colors found deeper within the Tabernacle appear at the threshold.

The entrance already corresponds to the character of the dwelling it leads toward.

The court is bounded, yet God gives an actual way in.

**Boundary and approach belong together.**

The gate establishes where movement toward the Presence begins.

The worshipper does not choose an entrance from any direction. The way of approach is part of the revealed order.

### v20–21 — Pure Oil Keeps the Light Burning

Then the LORD commands Israel:

> “that they bring thee pure oil olive beaten for the light, to cause the lamp to burn always.”

The chapter now moves from the outer court back toward the inner light.

The oil must be:

> **“pure.”**

Its purpose is:

> **“for the light.”**

And the light must:

> **“burn always.”**

The lamp is therefore sustained through prepared oil brought by the people and tended by the priesthood.

Aaron and his sons are to order it:

> “from evening to morning before the LORD.”

The light is kept **before God**.

This gives the chapter its final movement.

What is surrendered at the altar, gathered within the court, and brought through the appointed way is finally ordered toward a Presence in which **light is continually maintained**.

The disciple needs more than a moment of illumination. The sacred interior must be kept lit.

Exodus has already taught this rhythm through daily manna and the steady hands of Moses. Here the same principle appears in the sanctuary:

**the light must be sustained.**

## Fractal Notes & References

Exodus 27 continues the outward movement of the sanctuary pattern. Exodus 25 began at the center with the Ark, Mercy Seat, bread, and light. Exodus 26 built the dwelling around that center. Exodus 27 establishes the outer court and altar, giving the worshipper an ordered field of approach.

The movement is therefore:

**Presence  
→ dwelling  
→ court  
→ altar  
→ gate**

in the order of revelation,

while the worshipper approaches:

**gate  
→ court  
→ altar  
→ dwelling  
→ Presence.**

The altar also prepares a major biblical line that reaches its fullness in Jesus Christ. Sacrifice stands within the way of approach to God, and the New Testament presents Christ as the One whose offering establishes access into the divine Presence. The BKV interior reading follows that same structure: surrender is built into the life that draws near to God.

The continual lamp develops another line running through Scripture. Light appears at creation, distinguishes Israel from Egypt during the plague of darkness, burns within the Tabernacle, and later becomes explicit in Christ’s declaration, “I am the light of the world.” Revelation brings the pattern to completion in the city illuminated by the glory of God.

Exodus 27 therefore keeps the chapter’s movement clear:

**what surrounds the Presence must be ordered through surrender, sacred boundary, appointed approach, and continual light.**

## Onomastics & Key Terms

| Name / Term | Textual / Linguistic Meaning | BKV Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Altar — Mizbeach** | From the Hebrew root associated with **sacrifice / slaughter** | **The appointed place where what is brought to God is surrendered and offered** |
| **Court — Chatser** | **Enclosure / courtyard** | **The bounded outer sacred field surrounding the dwelling** |
| **Gate — Sha'ar** | **Gate / entrance** | **The appointed point of entry into the sacred order** |
| **Horn — Qeren** | **Horn**, commonly associated in Scripture with strength or power | **Strength joined to the altar and brought into the work of sacrifice** |
| **Brass / Bronze — Nechosheth** | Copper or bronze | Functionally, **the dominant metal of the altar and outer court, marking the field of sacrifice and approach** |
| **Oil — Shemen** | Oil, here specifically olive oil | **Prepared substance used to sustain the sanctuary light** |
| **Pure — Zakh** | **Pure / clean / clear** | The quality required of the oil brought for continual illumination |
| **Lamp — Ner** | **Lamp / light** | **The maintained light burning before the LORD within the sacred dwelling** |

The chapter’s strongest physical sequence is simple:

**ALTAR**

**COURT**

**GATE**

**LIGHT**

The altar receives what is surrendered.

The court establishes where the sacred life is held.

The gate gives the appointed way of approach.

The oil keeps the light burning before God.

That gives Exodus 27 a coherent place within the Tabernacle sequence.

Exodus 25 established **what occupies the center**.

Exodus 26 established **how the dwelling is built around that center**.

Exodus 27 establishes **how the outer life approaches and supports that dwelling**.

The faculties that once spent themselves maintaining Pharaoh’s world are now being given a completely different outer order:

**their strength can be offered,  
their field can be bounded,  
their approach can be directed,  
and their light can be kept burning before the LORD.**
