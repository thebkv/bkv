# PROVERBS 1

## 01 — THE CRY IN THE STREETS

Wisdom calls openly to everyone willing to hear, but not everyone is willing to listen. Solomon begins by teaching that the fear of the Lord is the beginning of knowledge. He warns his son against those who promise easy riches through violence and dishonesty, then introduces Wisdom herself, crying aloud in the streets and calling men to turn from their foolishness. The disciple should recognize that the voice of wisdom is not always telling him what he wants to hear. Sometimes it comes as correction, exposing a path he has chosen that will eventually destroy him. The question is whether he will listen while there is still time to turn. Those who refuse wisdom must eventually face the consequences of their own choices, but those who receive her instruction are promised a life established in safety. The disciple must learn to recognize God's instruction, receive correction without resisting it, and walk in the way that leads to life.

In Proverbs 1:7, the ancient word translated “fear” is yir'ah (יִרְאָה), from a root associated with fear, trembling, reverence, and awe. It can describe actual fear of danger, but when directed toward the Lord, it also carries the sense of recognizing His greatness, authority, and holiness. The ancient meaning includes reverence, but it should not be reduced to mere respect.

For the disciple, the fear of the Lord begins when God becomes more real and authoritative to him than his own opinions, desires, or the approval of other people. He approaches God with awe, receives His correction, and takes His Word seriously. This is the beginning of wisdom because it places the disciple in a position to be taught.

The word yir'ah embraces both awe and accountability: the disciple recognizes God's goodness, but also understands that His authority is not something to treat lightly.


## The Savior Test

True wisdom can be found:

> "The fear of the LORD is the beginning of knowledge: but fools despise wisdom and instruction." — Proverbs 1:7

Jesus confirms the importance of fearing God:

> "But I will forewarn you whom ye shall fear: Fear him, which after he hath killed hath power to cast into hell; yea, I say unto you, Fear him." — Luke 12:5

The disciple must understand what this fear requires of him. He is learning to give God the place of highest authority in his life. He can no longer allow the opinions of other people, the demands of the world, or his own desires to take precedence over what God has revealed. The fear of the Lord begins when the disciple recognizes whose word he must ultimately obey.

Proverbs then warns against the invitation of sinners who promise gain through violence and dishonesty. They ask the young man to join them, share their purse, and participate in their schemes. But the father warns him not to walk in their way, because they are laying a trap for their own lives.

Jesus gives the disciple the same warning about the way he chooses to follow:

> "Enter ye in at the strait gate: for wide is the gate, and broad is the way, that leadeth to destruction, and many there be which go in thereat." — Matthew 7:13

The danger in Proverbs is not simply that wicked men exist. It is that they invite the disciple to join them. They offer belonging, wealth, and the appearance of advantage. Jesus tells him to consider where the road leads. A way may attract many followers and seem profitable at first, yet still lead to destruction.

Then Proverbs introduces Wisdom as a woman crying openly in the streets. She calls to the simple, the scorners, and the fools, asking how long they will continue loving their foolishness.

Her invitation is remarkable:

> "Turn you at my reproof: behold, I will pour out my spirit unto you, I will make known my words unto you." — Proverbs 1:23

Jesus gives a closely related invitation:

> "Repent: for the kingdom of heaven is at hand." — Matthew 4:17

Both calls require the hearer to turn. Wisdom is not merely offering interesting information. She is calling men to abandon the way that has been destroying them and receive instruction that will lead them into life.

Jesus also makes an extraordinary promise concerning his words:

> "The words that I speak unto you, they are spirit, and they are life." — John 6:63

This gives the disciple a deeper understanding of Wisdom's invitation. The words of Jesus carry the life of the Spirit. He is not simply telling the disciple what is right. He is calling him into a living relationship with the truth, where the Word he receives begins to transform his thinking and conduct.

But Proverbs also tells us what happens when men continually reject Wisdom's call. They refuse her counsel, disregard her correction, and eventually find themselves overwhelmed by the consequences of the path they have chosen.

Jesus confirms this warning when he speaks over Jerusalem:

> "How often would I have gathered thy children together, even as a hen gathereth her chickens under her wings, and ye would not!" — Matthew 23:37

Here the connection is especially strong. Wisdom has called, but the people have refused. Jesus has offered to gather Jerusalem beneath his protection, but they would not come. In both passages, the tragedy is that the invitation was given and rejected.

The disciple should consider how he responds when the truth corrects him. Does he receive instruction, or does he defend the old way because it is familiar? Does he recognize the voice of the Savior when his words expose something that must change?

Proverbs ends with a promise:

> "But whoso hearkeneth unto me shall dwell safely, and shall be quiet from fear of evil." — Proverbs 1:33

Jesus gives the disciple a beautiful picture of this security:

> "Therefore whosoever heareth these sayings of mine, and doeth them, I will liken him unto a wise man, which built his house upon a rock." — Matthew 7:24

The wise man hears and obeys. When the storms come, his house stands because it has been founded upon the rock.

The disciple can now see the whole movement of Proverbs 1. Wisdom calls him away from the broad road, invites him to receive correction, and promises safety to those who listen. Jesus confirms every part of that movement. He calls men to repentance, teaches them the way of life, and shows them how to build upon a foundation that will endure.

The disciple must ask himself whether he has merely admired wisdom or has begun to obey it. Has he listened when the Word corrected him? Has he turned away from the companions and habits that would lead him into destruction? Has he built his life upon what Jesus actually teaches?

Wisdom has cried in the streets. Jesus has spoken the words of life.

The disciple must hear and follow.
