# Galatians 6 — The Harvest of the New Creation

## BKV Insight

Galatians 6 shows what life in the Spirit looks like when it begins to take form in actual human life. Paul has just told the Galatians to walk in the Spirit and has described the fruit that the Spirit produces. Now he brings that teaching into their relationships, their choices, their endurance, and finally their understanding of what Christ has made them.

Someone is overtaken in a fault. Restore him gently.

Someone is carrying a heavy burden. Help him carry it.

Examine your own work.

Keep doing good.

And pay attention to what you are planting, because:

> "Whatsoever a man soweth, that shall he also reap."
> — Galatians 6:7

This gives Paul a simple image for something he has been explaining throughout the letter. A seed carries a future within it. What is sown into the flesh eventually produces according to the flesh. What is sown into the Spirit produces according to the Spirit.

The harvest takes time. Paul therefore tells them not to grow weary. There is a season between planting and reaping when the seed is already at work and the harvest has not yet appeared.

Then Paul brings the letter back to the controversy that started it. Some want circumcision in the flesh to establish the Galatians' standing before God. Paul points to something much deeper that has already happened through Christ:

> "For in Christ Jesus neither circumcision availeth any thing, nor uncircumcision, but a new creature."
> — Galatians 6:15

The governing reality is **new creation**.

This is where the whole chapter has been heading. Life in the Spirit restores, carries, gives, sows, perseveres, and eventually bears fruit because a new life is already present.

The pattern is:

**LIFE IN THE SPIRIT → SOWING → PATIENT GROWTH → HARVEST → NEW CREATION REVEALED**

The harvest reveals the life that was growing all along.


## Canonical History & Context

Galatians 6 concludes Paul's argument about whether Gentile believers must receive circumcision and come under the Mosaic law in order to belong fully to God's covenant people.

Paul has spent the letter moving the Galatians away from confidence in what can be established through the flesh and toward life through faith in Christ and the Spirit.

That argument reaches an important point in chapter 5:

> "Walk in the Spirit, and ye shall not fulfil the lust of the flesh."
> — Galatians 5:16

Paul then describes the works of the flesh and the fruit of the Spirit. Chapter 6 continues directly from there.

The question now becomes: What does this Spirit-led life actually look like among people?

It looks like restoring someone who has fallen without becoming proud yourself. It looks like helping another person carry a burden. It requires examining your own work rather than measuring yourself against someone else. It means sharing good things, continuing to do good, and sowing toward a harvest that may take time to appear.

Paul then returns to circumcision one final time. Some people want a visible mark in the flesh. Paul points instead to the cross of Christ and declares that the reality that now governs everything is **new creation**.

Galatians therefore ends where its argument has been moving from the beginning: the life God gives through Christ must become the life by which the disciple actually lives.


## Natural Reading

### Galatians 6:1 — Restore What Has Fallen

#### What Happens

Paul begins with a believer who has been overtaken in a fault. Those who are spiritual are told to restore that person in a spirit of meekness while also watching themselves.

#### The Key

The word translated **restore** is *katartizō*. It can describe putting something back into proper condition, setting something right, or mending what has been damaged.

That gives us the governing action of the verse.

The spiritual person restores.

Paul also tells the person doing the restoring to consider himself. Meekness grows naturally from knowing that the same human weakness exists in the person offering help.

#### BKV

This is the first concrete picture Paul gives after describing the fruit of the Spirit in chapter 5.

Life in the Spirit moves toward restoration.

The same pattern works within the disciple. A habit, reaction, desire, fear, or old way of thinking can overtake us and pull part of life out of alignment. The Spirit works to restore that part of life to the government of Christ.

What has fallen is brought back into order.


### Galatians 6:2–5 — Carry the Burden

> "Bear ye one another's burdens, and so fulfil the law of Christ."
> — Galatians 6:2

#### What Happens

Paul tells the Galatians to bear one another's burdens. He also tells each person to examine his own work and says that every person will bear his own burden.

These statements describe two parts of life together.

#### The Key

Paul uses different Greek words in these verses.

In verse 2, *barē* refers to **burdens or heavy weights**.

In verse 5, *phortion* refers to a **load or responsibility carried by a person**.

There are heavy weights that love helps another person carry, and there is also a responsibility that belongs to each person's own walk before God.

Paul calls bearing one another's burdens the fulfillment of **the law of Christ**.

The life of the Spirit therefore takes shape as love expressed through actual responsibility for one another.

#### BKV

The Spirit joins personal responsibility with shared life.

Each disciple has a path to walk, work to examine, and a load that belongs to him. Yet the strength given to one member can become help for another member who is carrying something too heavy alone.

This is Christ taking form within a community.

The life within one person moves outward to strengthen another.


### Galatians 6:6 — Share What Is Good

#### What Happens

Paul tells the person receiving instruction in the word to share good things with the person teaching him.

#### The Key

The word translated **communicate** is *koinōneō*. It carries the idea of sharing, participating, or having fellowship in something.

The person who receives becomes a participant in giving.

This prepares naturally for Paul's image of sowing and reaping. What has been received begins to move outward.

#### BKV

Spiritual life circulates.

Truth received becomes truth lived. Good received becomes good shared. What God gives enters a person and then begins to move through that person into the lives of others.

The field is already being planted.


### Galatians 6:7–8 — The Seed and Its Harvest

> "Be not deceived; God is not mocked: for whatsoever a man soweth, that shall he also reap."
> — Galatians 6:7

#### What Happens

Paul gives the Galatians an agricultural principle everyone could understand.

A person harvests according to what he plants.

Paul then identifies two directions of sowing: **the flesh** and **the Spirit**.

The person who sows to the flesh reaps corruption from the flesh. The person who sows to the Spirit reaps everlasting life from the Spirit.

#### The Key

The image depends upon correspondence between seed and harvest.

The harvest reveals the nature of what was planted.

This follows an order established at the beginning of Scripture:

> "Let the earth bring forth grass, the herb yielding seed, and the fruit tree yielding fruit after his kind, whose seed is in itself..."
> — Genesis 1:11

Seed carries within itself the pattern of what it will produce.

Paul applies that familiar order to human life. What a person repeatedly gives himself to becomes a kind of sowing. Eventually the field produces according to the seed it has received.

#### BKV

This gives us a clear picture of how the inner life becomes visible.

Thoughts, desires, words, choices, habits, attention, and action all participate in sowing. They place something into the field of life that can grow.

Sowing to the flesh strengthens the life organized around the natural self and its desires.

Sowing to the Spirit gives place to the life of God within.

The harvest may appear later, yet it carries the nature of its seed.

A different harvest begins with different seed.


### Galatians 6:9 — The Season Between Seed and Harvest

> "And let us not be weary in well doing: for in due season we shall reap, if we faint not."
> — Galatians 6:9

#### What Happens

Paul tells the Galatians to continue doing good because the harvest will come in its proper season.

#### The Key

The phrase translated **due season** is *kairō idiō* — its own proper or appointed time.

That means Paul's image contains an interval.

The seed is planted first.

Growth follows.

The harvest appears in its season.

Paul's instruction concerns that middle period, when the person has already sown and still cannot see the completed result.

Keep going.

#### BKV

This gives us one of the chapter's clearest transformation patterns:

**SEED → HIDDEN GROWTH → APPOINTED SEASON → HARVEST**

There are times when a disciple has genuinely begun sowing to the Spirit while the visible conditions still resemble the old field.

The seed is already present.

Growth is already underway.

The instruction is to continue until what has been planted reaches its season.

Transformation often becomes visible after it has already been growing for some time.


### Galatians 6:10 — The Field Around Us

#### What Happens

Paul brings the principle directly into everyday life. Whenever opportunity appears, believers are to do good to everyone, with particular care for the household of faith.

#### The Key

Sowing to the Spirit is taking place in ordinary life.

The opportunities are already present in the people and circumstances around them.

#### BKV

Everyday life becomes the field.

A word can become seed. Generosity can become seed. Mercy can become seed. Restoring someone can become seed. Helping another person carry a burden can become seed.

The earlier instructions in the chapter now fit naturally into Paul's agricultural image.

The harvest grows from a life repeatedly given to the Spirit.


### Galatians 6:11–13 — The Visible Mark

#### What Happens

Paul returns to the controversy that has run through the letter.

Some people are pressuring the Galatians to receive circumcision. Paul says they want to make "a fair shew in the flesh" and avoid persecution connected with the cross of Christ.

#### The Key

Circumcision gives Paul a concrete example of the larger issue he has been addressing.

It is a visible mark placed upon the flesh.

Paul has spent Galatians directing attention toward something happening much deeper: Christ living within the believer and the believer walking by the Spirit.

The question becomes one of source.

What is producing this life?

#### BKV

The natural governing mind looks for something visible that can establish identity and righteousness.

Paul has already given the Galatians another test: look at the fruit.

A life governed by the Spirit eventually reveals the Spirit that produced it.

The decisive question is therefore larger than the mark carried in the flesh.

**What life is growing here?**

Paul answers that question in the next verses.


### Galatians 6:14–15 — The New Creation

> "But God forbid that I should glory, save in the cross of our Lord Jesus Christ..."
> — Galatians 6:14

Then Paul gives the declaration toward which the letter has been moving:

> "For in Christ Jesus neither circumcision availeth any thing, nor uncircumcision, but a new creature."
> — Galatians 6:15

#### What Happens

Paul declares that his glory is in the cross of Jesus Christ. Through that cross, the world has been crucified to him and he to the world.

Then he names the reality that now matters:

**new creation.**

#### The Key

The Greek phrase is *kainē ktisis* — **new creation**.

Paul is describing something deeper than a change in religious status or outward behavior. The cross has ended an old governing relationship, and a new life has begun.

This reaches back to something Paul said much earlier in the letter:

> "I am crucified with Christ: nevertheless I live; yet not I, but Christ liveth in me..."
> — Galatians 2:20

That statement gives Galatians 6:15 its depth.

The new creation is life whose source is now Christ.

#### BKV

Here the agricultural pattern reaches its full meaning.

Paul has spoken about the flesh and the Spirit, seed and harvest, perseverance and due season. Now he names the life toward which the whole movement points.

**New creation.**

The cross marks the end of the old governing order. The separated self, whose identity is built from what it can accomplish, possess, display, and establish through the flesh, loses its place as the center.

Christ becomes the living center.

The movement running through Galatians can therefore be seen as:

**CROSS → LIFE IN THE SPIRIT → FRUIT → NEW CREATION**

And within this chapter:

**SOWING → GROWTH → HARVEST**

These are two scales of the same pattern. The life of Christ planted within the disciple grows until the nature of that life becomes visible.

The harvest reveals the new creation already being formed.


### Galatians 6:16–18 — The Marks of Jesus

> "And as many as walk according to this rule, peace be on them, and mercy..."
> — Galatians 6:16

#### What Happens

Paul blesses those who walk according to this rule with peace and mercy.

Then he makes a deeply personal statement:

> "From henceforth let no man trouble me: for I bear in my body the marks of the Lord Jesus."
> — Galatians 6:17

He closes the letter with the grace of Jesus Christ.

#### The Key

The word translated **marks** is *stigmata*, meaning marks or scars upon the body.

Paul has just discussed people who want circumcision as a mark in the flesh. Paul himself carries marks produced by his suffering in the service of Christ.

His body bears the history of the life he has actually lived.

That gives the ending considerable force.

#### BKV

Paul finishes with something embodied.

The life he has described throughout the letter has passed through actual suffering, actual faithfulness, and actual human experience. His body carries the evidence.

This completes the movement of the chapter.

The Spirit becomes fruit.

Seed becomes harvest.

The inward life becomes embodied life.

Christ takes form in the disciple.

And Paul gives that completed reality its governing name:

**new creation.**


## Fractal Notes & References

### Seed → Fruit → Harvest

The seed pattern begins in Genesis:

**Genesis 1:11–12**  
The earth brings forth plants and trees whose seed is within them, producing after their kind.

**Psalm 1:3**  
The righteous person is pictured as a tree planted by rivers of water that brings forth fruit in its season.

**Matthew 7:16–20**  
Jesus says a tree is known by its fruit.

**Galatians 5:22–23**  
Paul describes the fruit of the Spirit.

**Galatians 6:7–9**  
What a person sows eventually becomes harvest.

Across these passages the same basic structure appears:

**SOURCE → GROWTH → FRUIT**

The visible fruit reveals the life operating within.


### Cross → New Creation

Paul develops another repeated movement within Galatians itself.

**Galatians 2:20**  
"I am crucified with Christ."

**Galatians 5:24**  
Those who belong to Christ have crucified the flesh with its affections and lusts.

**Galatians 6:14**  
The world is crucified to Paul and Paul to the world.

**Galatians 6:15**  
The governing reality is new creation.

The movement is:

**CRUCIFIXION → LIFE IN THE SPIRIT → NEW CREATION**

The cross removes the old governing center so that the life of Christ can become the source of the disciple's life.


### The Hidden Season

Paul's "due season" also participates in a repeated biblical pattern.

Joseph receives dreams long before he governs Egypt.

David is anointed long before he sits upon the throne.

Israel receives the promise of the land before entering it.

Jesus describes seed entering the ground and growing toward harvest.

Paul tells the Galatians to continue sowing because harvest arrives in its appointed season.

The repeated movement is:

**SEED / PROMISE → GROWTH / PREPARATION → APPEARANCE**

The interval belongs to the process.

What will eventually become visible may already be alive beneath the surface.


## Onomastics

| **Christ — Christos** | Anointed One | 6:2, 12, 14, 15, 18 | The governing life taking form in the believer |

| **Spirit — Pneuma** | Spirit, breath, wind | 6:1, 8, 18 | The divine life from which the new harvest grows |
| **Flesh — Sarx** | Flesh; in this argument, natural human life operating as its own governing source | 6:8, 12, 13 | The field whose own life produces according to its nature |
| **New Creation — Kainē Ktisis** | New creation; something newly brought into being | 6:15 | Paul's culminating description of life in Christ |


Galatians 6 begins with a person who needs to be restored and ends with a new creation.

Between those two points, Paul shows us what life in the Spirit looks like as it takes form.

The Spirit restores what has fallen. It gives strength to carry another person's burden. It teaches the disciple to examine his own work, to share what he has received, and to keep sowing good seed even while the field still appears unchanged.

Then the harvest comes in its season.

Paul's final answer to the controversy in Galatia is therefore much larger than circumcision. The question is what kind of life has actually been brought forth.

The cross has opened the way for a new governing life.

Christ lives within the disciple.

The Spirit becomes fruit.

The seed becomes harvest.

And what was growing within finally appears as **new creation**.
