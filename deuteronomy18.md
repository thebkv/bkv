## The Savior Test

Moses gives Israel a remarkable promise:

> "I will raise them up a Prophet from among their brethren, like unto thee, and will put my words in his mouth; and he shall speak unto them all that I shall command him." — Deuteronomy 18:18

Jesus confirms the nature of his own mission:

> "For I have not spoken of myself; but the Father which sent me, he gave me a commandment, what I should say, and what I should speak." — John 12:49

Notice how closely these words correspond. Moses says that God will raise up a Prophet and give him the words he must speak. Jesus tells us that the Father sent him and commanded him what to say. Jesus is describing the very relationship Moses foretold.

Jesus also tells those who claimed to believe Moses:

> "For had ye believed Moses, ye would have believed me: for he wrote of me." — John 5:46

The disciple should understand what Jesus is saying. Moses was preparing Israel to receive someone greater than himself. The law and the prophets were leading toward Christ, and those who truly received the testimony of Moses should have recognized the one to whom he pointed.

Then, on the mountain of Transfiguration, the Father gives the decisive command:

> "This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased; hear ye him." — Matthew 17:5

Moses had said, "Unto him ye shall hearken." Now the Father identifies Jesus and says, "Hear ye him."

The connection becomes even stronger when we turn to Peter's sermon in Acts 3. After healing the lame man at the Beautiful Gate, Peter speaks to Israel about Jesus Christ and quotes Moses directly:

> "For Moses truly said unto the fathers, A prophet shall the Lord your God raise up unto you of your brethren, like unto me; him shall ye hear in all things whatsoever he shall say unto you." — Acts 3:22

Peter then continues:

> "And it shall come to pass, that every soul, which will not hear that prophet, shall be destroyed from among the people." — Acts 3:23

This removes any uncertainty about how Peter understood the prophecy. He identifies Jesus as the promised Prophet and tells Israel that Moses' command to listen to him must now be fulfilled.

We therefore have three witnesses to the meaning of Deuteronomy 18. Jesus declares that he speaks the words given him by the Father. The Father identifies Jesus as his beloved Son and commands the disciples to hear him. Peter explicitly applies Moses' prophecy to Jesus Christ.

But Deuteronomy 18 also warns against false prophets, and Jesus confirms that warning:

> "Beware of false prophets, which come to you in sheep's clothing, but inwardly they are ravening wolves." — Matthew 7:15

Moses warned Israel against those who spoke presumptuously in God's name. Jesus tells his disciples to examine the fruit of those who claim spiritual authority. A man may speak confidently, make impressive claims, or appear righteous, yet still lead others away from the truth.

The disciple must learn to test what he hears against the teaching of Jesus. Does it agree with the character of the Father whom Jesus reveals? Does it lead him toward obedience, mercy, truthfulness, and love? Or does it make him dependent upon another person's supposed authority, secret knowledge, or predictions about the future?

This is the great lesson of Deuteronomy 18. Israel was forbidden to seek spiritual direction from divination, familiar spirits, and those who claimed forbidden knowledge. Instead, God promised to raise up a Prophet through whom He would speak.

That Prophet has now been revealed.

The disciple should ask himself whose voice he has been following. He may have spent years listening to religious authorities, accumulating interpretations, or searching for someone who claims to know the hidden purposes of God. But the Father has already given him the command that settles the matter.

**"Hear ye him."**

The disciple must come to Jesus, receive his words, and put them into practice. The Prophet promised through Moses has come, and the Father himself has told us whom to hear.
