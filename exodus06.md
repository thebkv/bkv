# Exodus 6 — The Promise of Deliverance Is Reaffirmed

## BKV Insight

Exodus 6 answers the crisis created in Exodus 5: **when the disciple's first movement toward freedom appears to fail, God redirects attention from the strength of Pharaoh, the discouragement of the faculties, and the inadequacy of the deliverer back to His own identity and action**. The repeated divine declaration is “I will”: God will bring out, deliver, redeem, take, establish relationship, and bring His people into the promised inheritance. The disciple is therefore taught that liberation does not finally depend upon the apparent strength of the enslaved faculties or the confidence of the human instrument, but upon **the LORD who remembers His covenant and acts to fulfill it**.

## Canonical History & Context

Exodus 6 begins immediately after Moses' complaint that his first confrontation with Pharaoh has only made Israel's condition worse. God answers that Pharaoh will indeed let Israel go under the pressure of God's mighty hand. He identifies Himself as the LORD, recalls His covenant with Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, and gives Moses a series of promises describing what He will do for Israel. Moses reports these words to the Israelites, but they cannot listen because of “anguish of spirit” and cruel bondage. God nevertheless commands Moses to return to Pharaoh. Moses again protests his inability to speak effectively. The narrative then pauses to give the genealogy of Reuben, Simeon, and especially Levi, establishing the lineage of Moses and Aaron before repeating their commission to bring Israel out of Egypt.

## Natural Reading

### v1 — The Answer to Apparent Failure

Then the LORD said unto Moses:

> “Now shalt thou see what I will do to Pharaoh.”

Exodus 5 ended with Moses saying:

> “Neither hast thou delivered thy people at all.”

God answers by redirecting Moses' attention:

**“Now shalt thou see what I will do.”**

The visible condition has become worse, but the outcome has not changed.

The strength of Pharaoh has now been fully exposed so that deliverance can be recognized as **the work of God rather than the achievement of Moses**.

For with a strong hand shall Pharaoh let them go, and with a strong hand shall he drive them out of his land.

The ruler who presently refuses to release the faculties will eventually become unable to retain them.

### v2–5 — “I Am the LORD”

And God spake unto Moses and said:

> **“I am the LORD.”**

Before God explains what He will do, He establishes **who He is**. He identifies Himself as the God who appeared unto **Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob**, and who established His covenant to give them the land of Canaan.

And He says:

> “I have also heard the groaning of the children of Israel, whom the Egyptians keep in bondage; and I have remembered my covenant.”

The present condition of bondage does not cancel the deeper covenantal identity of Israel.

Pharaoh sees:

**slaves.**

God sees:

**the people of the covenant.**

The faculties may have forgotten who they are; God has not.

### v6–8 — The Sevenfold “I Will”

Therefore say unto the children of **Israel — God-Prevails**:

> **“I am the LORD.”**

Then comes the great sequence of divine action:

**I WILL bring you out** from under the burdens of Egypt.

**I WILL rid you out of their bondage.**

**I WILL redeem you** with a stretched out arm.

**I WILL take you to me for a people.**

**I WILL be to you a God.**

**I WILL bring you in** unto the land promised to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob.

**I WILL give it you** for an heritage.

The chapter places the weight of deliverance upon:

# I WILL.

The faculties are not being asked to invent their own escape from bondage.

God Himself declares the movement:

**OUT OF BURDENS  
→ OUT OF BONDAGE  
→ REDEEMED  
→ TAKEN TO GOD  
→ GOD KNOWN  
→ BROUGHT INTO THE LAND  
→ INHERITANCE RECEIVED**

Deliverance is therefore much larger than leaving Egypt.

Its purpose is restored **relationship, identity, and inheritance**.

And the sequence begins and ends with the same declaration:

> **“I am the LORD.”**

### v9 — The Faculties Cannot Yet Hear

And Moses spake so unto the children of Israel:

> “but they hearkened not unto Moses for anguish of spirit, and for cruel bondage.”

God has just given an extraordinary promise, but the enslaved faculties **cannot receive it**. Bondage has become so severe that it affects their capacity to hear the message of deliverance.

Their inability to believe does not cancel the promise. God's action is not dependent upon their present emotional capacity to imagine freedom. The promise stands even while the faculties remain **too crushed to hear it**.

### v10–12 — Moses Again Sees His Own Inadequacy

And the LORD tells Moses:

> “Go in, speak unto Pharaoh king of Egypt, that he let the children of Israel go out of his land.”

But Moses answers:

> “Behold, the children of Israel have not hearkened unto me; how then shall Pharaoh hear me?”

The delivering consciousness looks at the evidence:

**Israel did not listen.  
Pharaoh is stronger.  
My speech is inadequate.**

Moses calls himself:

> **“of uncircumcised lips.”**

His own faculty of expression still appears insufficient for the task.

But the divine commission does not rest upon Moses' estimate of his effectiveness.

God has already said:

> **“I will.”**

### v13 — The Command Remains

And the LORD spake unto Moses and unto Aaron and gave them:

> “a charge unto the children of Israel, and unto Pharaoh king of Egypt, to bring the children of Israel out of the land of Egypt.”

Israel's discouragement, Moses' insecurity, and Pharaoh's resistance do not change the charge. The divine purpose remains fixed:

**BRING ISRAEL OUT.**

### v14–19 — The Lineage Is Recalled

Now the narrative pauses and begins naming the houses of Israel.

First:

**Reuben — See, a Son / Seeing**

Then:

**Simeon — Hearing**

Then:

**Levi — Joining**

The genealogy moves toward the tribe from which Moses and Aaron arise.

At the moment when Moses doubts his adequacy, Scripture places him back inside a **lineage**. The deliverer is not an isolated individual inventing a private mission. He stands within the continuing structure of **Israel — God-Prevails** and within **Levi — Joining**, the house from which the priestly and delivering work now emerges.

### v20 — Amram, Jochebed, Aaron, and Moses

And **Amram** took him **Jochebed** his father's sister to wife.

And she bare him:

**Aaron and Moses.**

The genealogy has now reached the two faculties God has joined for the work:

**Moses — the Drawn-Out One**

and

**Aaron — the speaking faculty joined to him.**

Their identity is being anchored before the confrontation resumes.

### v21–25 — The House Expands

The genealogy continues through the descendants of Levi and Aaron.

Among them appear names that will matter greatly later:

**Korah**

**Nadab**

**Abihu**

**Eleazar**

**Phinehas**

The coming Exodus is already larger than Moses.

Within these names lie future priesthood, service, conflict, judgment, and continuity.

The deliverance of the faculties is preparing an entire **ordered people**, not merely rescuing isolated individuals.

### v26–27 — “These Are That Aaron and Moses”

Then Scripture stops and identifies them directly:

> **“These are that Aaron and Moses.”**

These are the men to whom the LORD said:

> “Bring out the children of Israel from the land of Egypt according to their armies.”

And:

> **“These are that Moses and Aaron.”**

The repetition establishes identity. The same Moses who has said:

**Who am I?**  
**They will not believe me.**  
**I am not eloquent.**  
**Israel has not listened to me.**

is still:

**THAT MOSES**

whom God has commissioned. Human uncertainty does not alter divine appointment.

### v28–30 — The Chapter Returns to the Commission

And the LORD says again:

> **“I am the LORD.”**

And commands Moses to speak unto Pharaoh all that He tells him.

And Moses again answers:

> “Behold, I am of uncircumcised lips, and how shall Pharaoh hearken unto me?”

The chapter deliberately ends with the tension unresolved:

**I AM THE LORD.**

against Moses' continuing:

**I AM INADEQUATE.**

This is the confrontation beneath the confrontation. Before Moses can stand securely before Pharaoh, the delivering consciousness must learn which statement governs the work.

Exodus 6 has already given the answer:

**I AM THE LORD.**

**I WILL BRING YOU OUT.**

## Fractal Notes & References

Exodus 6 reveals a recurring biblical pattern in which **God's promise becomes most explicit precisely when visible circumstances appear to contradict it**. Abraham receives covenant promises while the fulfillment remains unseen; Joseph's calling survives slavery and imprisonment; Israel receives the sevenfold “I will” while still crushed beneath Pharaoh; and throughout Scripture the divine word precedes the manifested condition it announces. The chapter also continues the Exodus fractal already forming across the book: in Exodus 1 the faculties enter bondage; in Exodus 2 the delivering consciousness is drawn out; in Exodus 3 it turns toward God; in Exodus 4 the call becomes embodied; in Exodus 5 the old order resists; and in Exodus 6 **God reestablishes the entire movement upon His own covenant and action**. The center has shifted decisively from what Moses can accomplish to what the LORD has declared He will do.

The genealogy is especially significant because of where it appears.

Moses has just said, in effect:

**Israel will not hear me.  
Pharaoh will not hear me.  
My own mouth is inadequate.**

Scripture responds by locating him again within:

**ISRAEL → LEVI → AMRAM + JOCHEBED → MOSES + AARON**

The delivering consciousness is reminded that it did not originate with itself.

It has a lineage.

It has a covenant.

And above the entire genealogy stands the repeated declaration:

> **“I am the LORD.”**

The identity of the instrument is secured by **the identity of the One who sends it**.
