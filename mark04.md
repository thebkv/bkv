# MARK 4

## 04 — THE SECRET OF THE SEED

Jesus begins beside the sea, teaching a multitude from a ship. He tells them about a sower whose seed falls on different kinds of ground. Some is taken away immediately, some springs up without sufficient root, some is choked by thorns, and some grows into a harvest of thirty, sixty, and a hundredfold. Later, when the disciples ask him about the parable, Jesus tells them plainly that the sower sows the Word. The ground is the condition of the heart receiving it.

This is where the disciple must begin. The same Word is offered to every kind of ground, but what happens afterward depends upon how it is received. A man may hear a truth and immediately lose it to the old thoughts that have always governed him. He may receive it with joy, only to abandon it when difficulty comes. He may even believe it, but allow the cares of this world, the deceitfulness of riches, and the desire for other things to crowd it out. Jesus is showing us why hearing the Word and bearing its fruit are not always the same experience. The disciple must give the Word a place to take root and remain with it until it begins to change his life.

Jesus then speaks of a candle that is meant to be placed upon a candlestick rather than hidden beneath a vessel or a bed. The truth received within is meant to become visible. He also tells them to take heed what they hear, because the measure they use will be measured back to them. The disciple should pay attention to what he allows into his heart and what he does with the truth he has already received. Understanding grows as it is faithfully used.

Then Jesus gives a remarkable parable found only in Mark. A man casts seed into the ground, sleeps and rises night after night, and the seed grows though he does not know how. First comes the blade, then the ear, and afterward the full corn in the ear. This is a great encouragement to the disciple who is trying to understand how spiritual life develops. He can receive the Word, keep it, and remain faithful to it, but he cannot force it to produce life by anxious effort. There is a living power at work within the seed itself. His part is to prepare the ground, receive what has been planted, and allow the growth to continue.

The mustard seed shows how something that appears almost insignificant can grow far beyond its beginning. A small measure of living faith may seem like very little when compared with the difficulties before a man, but Jesus is teaching that the kingdom carries within itself the power to grow into something far greater than its first appearance.

That evening, Jesus tells the disciples to pass over to the other side of the sea. A great storm arises, and the waves begin filling the ship. Jesus is asleep upon a pillow in the hinder part of the vessel. The disciples awaken him, frightened that they are about to perish. He rises, rebukes the wind, and commands the sea, "Peace, be still." The wind ceases, and there is a great calm.

Here the teaching becomes a living demonstration. Jesus has spent the day explaining the Word that grows within a man, and now the disciples must discover whether they can trust that Word in the middle of a storm. Jesus has given the command to cross over, yet they are terrified by what happens along the way. The disciple will recognize this experience. He may have received a genuine spiritual understanding, only to find himself overcome by fear when circumstances appear to contradict it. The storm is real, just as the storm on Galilee was real, but the presence and authority of Christ are greater than the storm.

Jesus asks them why they are so fearful and how it is that they have no faith. They have witnessed the miracle, but they are still learning who is with them in the ship. The chapter ends with their astonished question: "What manner of man is this, that even the wind and the sea obey him?"

## The Savior Test

Jesus gives the interpretation himself:

> "The sower soweth the word." — Mark 4:14

And later:

> "So is the kingdom of God, as if a man should cast seed into the ground; And should sleep, and rise night and day, and the seed should spring and grow up, he knoweth not how." — Mark 4:26–27

These are the keys to the chapter. Jesus is teaching that the kingdom begins with the living Word received into the heart, and that its growth is the work of a power greater than the disciple's own effort.

When the storm comes, Jesus asks:

> "Why are ye so fearful? how is it that ye have no faith?" — Mark 4:40

The miracle confirms what the parables have been teaching. The Word that produces life within the disciple is also the Word that commands the wind and the sea. Jesus does not merely describe divine power. He exercises it before their eyes.

The disciple should therefore examine the ground of his own heart. Has he allowed the Word to take root? Is he giving it room to grow, or is he surrendering it whenever fear and worldly cares arise? And when the storm comes, does he remember who is with him in the ship?

The same Jesus who plants the seed of the kingdom is the one who says, "Peace, be still." The disciple is learning to trust him through the whole journey, from the first appearance of the seed to the full harvest.
