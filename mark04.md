# MARK 4

## 04 — THE SECRET OF THE SEED

Jesus sits in a ship beside the shore, teaching the multitude gathered on the land. He tells them of a sower whose seed falls on different kinds of ground. Some is taken away immediately, some springs up but has no deep root, some is choked by thorns, and some grows into a harvest of thirty, sixty, and a hundredfold. When the disciples ask him what the parable means, Jesus explains that the sower sows the Word. The different kinds of ground show what happens when that Word enters the human heart.

This is where the disciple must begin. A man may hear a truth and immediately lose it to the old thoughts that have always governed him. He may receive it with joy, only to abandon it when difficulty comes. He may even believe it, yet allow the cares of this world, the deceitfulness of riches, and the desire for other things to crowd it out. Jesus is showing us why hearing the Word and bearing its fruit are often two different experiences. The disciple must give the Word a place to take root and remain with it until it begins to change his life.

Jesus then speaks of a candle that belongs on a candlestick rather than hidden beneath a vessel or a bed. What has been received within is meant to come into the light. He also tells them to take heed what they hear, because the measure they use will be measured back to them. The disciple must pay attention to what he receives, how he understands it, and what he does with it. The truth he faithfully receives and uses prepares him to receive more.

Then Jesus gives a remarkable parable found only in Mark. A man casts seed into the ground, sleeps and rises night after night, and the seed grows though he does not know how. First comes the blade, then the ear, and afterward the full corn in the ear. The man has planted the seed, but there is a power at work beyond his understanding. He cannot make the seed live by worrying over it, nor can he force the harvest to appear before its time.

This is a great encouragement to the disciple. He may receive the Word and remain faithful to it without yet seeing the change he desires. Jesus is telling him that the kingdom has a life of its own. The disciple must receive the seed into good ground, but he can trust the power of God to bring forth its fruit. He does not have to understand every stage of the growth to know that the Word is living and active within him.

The mustard seed carries the lesson further. What begins as something exceedingly small grows into something great enough for the birds of the air to lodge beneath its branches. The disciple should never despise the small beginning of faith. A truth that has only just taken root may seem insignificant beside the troubles of his life, yet the kingdom of God is already present in that beginning.

That evening, Jesus tells the disciples, "Let us pass over unto the other side." They take him in the ship, and a great storm arises. The wind beats against the vessel, the waves fill it with water, and the disciples fear they are about to perish. Yet Jesus is asleep upon a pillow in the hinder part of the ship.

There is something extraordinary about the way Mark has arranged these events. Jesus has just described a man who plants the seed and then sleeps while it grows. Now Jesus himself sleeps while a storm threatens the ship. In both scenes, something is happening that the natural mind cannot control. The sleeping man cannot explain how the seed grows, and the frightened disciples cannot see how they will survive the storm. Yet Jesus is at rest.

The disciples awaken him and ask, "Master, carest thou not that we perish?" Jesus rises, rebukes the wind, and speaks to the sea: "Peace, be still." The wind ceases, and there is a great calm.

Here the teaching becomes a living demonstration. Jesus has spent the day showing the disciples how the Word works, and now they must discover whether they can trust that Word when everything around them appears to contradict it. Before they entered the storm, Jesus had already said, "Let us pass over unto the other side." They had his word concerning the journey. But when the wind arose and the water entered the ship, what they saw and felt became more convincing to them than what they had heard.

The disciple will recognize this experience. He may receive a genuine spiritual understanding and believe it while everything is peaceful, only to find himself overcome by fear when circumstances become threatening. The storm on Galilee was a real storm, and Jesus actually stilled it. But the event also reveals what happens within a man when fear begins to govern him. He forgets the Word he has received and judges his future by the danger immediately before him.

Jesus asks, "Why are ye so fearful? how is it that ye have no faith?" The disciples have seen the wind and sea obey him, yet they are still discovering the authority of the one who has been with them all along. The chapter ends with their astonished question: "What manner of man is this, that even the wind and the sea obey him?"

## The Savior Test

Jesus gives us the meaning of the seed himself:

> "The sower soweth the word." — Mark 4:14

And concerning its growth:

> "So is the kingdom of God, as if a man should cast seed into the ground; And should sleep, and rise night and day, and the seed should spring and grow up, he knoweth not how." — Mark 4:26–27

The kingdom begins with the living Word received into the heart. Its growth is the work of a power greater than the disciple's own effort. Jesus teaches him to receive the Word, keep it, and trust the life that is already working within it.

Then, before the storm, Jesus says:

> "Let us pass over unto the other side." — Mark 4:35

And afterward:

> "Why are ye so fearful? how is it that ye have no faith?" — Mark 4:40

The disciples had been given the Word before the danger appeared. Jesus' question brings them back to the very lesson he has been teaching. What will they believe when the circumstances before their eyes seem stronger than the promise they have received?

The miracle confirms the authority of the one who spoke the parables. The same Jesus who explains the living power of the seed commands the wind and the sea. He teaches the kingdom and demonstrates its power before their eyes.

The disciple should examine the ground of his own heart. Has he received the Word deeply enough to remain faithful when trouble comes? Can he allow God's work to grow without trying to force its progress? And when the storm rises, can he remember what Jesus has already said?

There is a beautiful connection between the sleeping man in the parable and the sleeping Savior in the ship. The disciple is learning that he can be at rest while the Word is at work. He can trust the growth he cannot yet see, and he can trust Christ when the waves rise around him.

Jesus said they were going to the other side. The disciple must learn to take him at his word.
