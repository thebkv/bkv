# Exodus 7 — Divine Authority Confronts the Ruling Power

## BKV Insight

Exodus 7 is the beginning of the **open contest for government of the disciple's faculties**. Moses no longer stands before Pharaoh as the uncertain man who once asked, “Who am I?”; he now acts under God's authority, while Aaron gives expression to the divine word. Pharaoh's magicians reveal that the old consciousness possesses real powers of imitation, yet their rods are swallowed and their imitation only reproduces the condition of judgment. The first plague then strikes the Nile itself: **God begins the liberation of the faculties by exposing and judging the source from which the ruling consciousness of bondage draws its life.**

## Canonical History & Context

Exodus 7 resumes God's answer to Moses' concern about his inability to speak. God tells Moses that he will stand before Pharaoh “as God,” while Aaron will serve as his prophet and spokesman. Moses and Aaron, now eighty and eighty-three years old, return to Pharaoh. Aaron casts down his rod and it becomes a serpent; Pharaoh's magicians apparently reproduce the sign, but Aaron's rod swallows theirs. Pharaoh nevertheless refuses to listen. God then sends Moses to confront Pharaoh at the Nile. Aaron stretches the rod over Egypt's waters, and the Nile and associated waters become blood. The fish die, the river stinks, and the Egyptians must dig for water. Pharaoh's magicians imitate the sign, Pharaoh's heart remains hardened, and seven days pass after the LORD strikes the river.

## Natural Reading

### v1–2 — The Delivering Consciousness Is Given Authority

And the LORD said unto **Moses — the Drawn-Out One**:

> “See, I have made thee a god to Pharaoh: and Aaron thy brother shall be thy prophet.”

The Moses who repeatedly questioned his adequacy is now placed before Pharaoh as the representative of **divine authority**.

And **Aaron — the faculty of expression joined to Moses** is to speak what Moses receives.

The order is now established:

**GOD → MOSES → AARON → PHARAOH**

What originates in God passes through the delivering consciousness, receives expression, and directly confronts the ruling power of bondage.

> “Thou shalt speak all that I command thee.”

The commission is no longer being negotiated; **it is being enacted**.

### v3–5 — Resistance Will Reveal God

And God says:

> “I will harden Pharaoh's heart, and multiply my signs and my wonders in the land of Egypt.”

Pharaoh's resistance will not prevent the divine work. It becomes the field upon which God's authority is progressively revealed.

And when Pharaoh refuses:

> “I will lay my hand upon Egypt, and bring forth mine armies, and my people the children of Israel.”

The faculties that Pharaoh regards as his labor force are now called:

> **“mine armies, and my people.”**

The conflict concerns rightful government.

And God declares the purpose:

> “The Egyptians shall know that I am the LORD.”

Pharaoh began the confrontation by asking:

> **“Who is the LORD?”**

The plagues will answer his question.

### v6–7 — Moses and Aaron Act

And Moses and Aaron did as the LORD commanded them:

> **“so did they.”**

The hesitation that characterized Moses' earlier chapters has given way to obedience. Moses is eighty years old and Aaron eighty-three when they speak unto Pharaoh. The deliverers enter the decisive confrontation after long formation; what matters now is **divine commission**.

### v8–10 — The Rod Becomes the Serpent

And the LORD tells Moses and Aaron that Pharaoh will demand a miracle.

Aaron is to cast his rod before Pharaoh.

And:

> “it became a serpent.”

The **rod of authority** again becomes the serpent.

But this time Moses does not flee from it.

The sign that frightened him in Exodus 4 is now performed deliberately **before Pharaoh**.

What the delivering consciousness first had to encounter within itself can now be carried into confrontation with the ruling power of bondage.

### v11–12 — The Counterfeit Can Imitate

Then Pharaoh called the wise men and sorcerers.

And the **magicians of Egypt** did likewise with their enchantments.

They cast down their rods:

> “and they became serpents.”

The old order is not powerless. It can produce **counterfeit correspondence** and imitate the outward appearance of the divine sign.

For the disciple, this requires discernment:

**similar manifestation does not necessarily mean identical source or authority.**

Then:

> **“Aaron's rod swallowed up their rods.”**

The contest is resolved not by appearance but by **superior authority**.

The powers of Egypt may imitate the sign.

They cannot survive incorporation into the authority proceeding from God.

### v13 — Pharaoh Remains Unmoved

And Pharaoh's heart was hardened:

> “that he hearkened not unto them.”

The sign has been given and the counterfeit overcome, yet Pharaoh does not yield. The problem is deeper than lack of evidence: the ruling consciousness is **fixed in its orientation**. Deliverance will therefore require the structure of Egypt itself to be confronted.

### v14–16 — God Meets Pharaoh at the River

And the LORD says:

> “Pharaoh's heart is hardened, he refuseth to let the people go.”

Moses is told to meet Pharaoh in the morning:

> “lo, he goeth out unto the water.”

The confrontation now moves to the **Nile**, the great sustaining river of Egypt.

And Moses is to say:

> “The LORD God of the Hebrews hath sent me unto thee, saying, Let my people go, that they may serve me in the wilderness.”

Again the purpose of deliverance is stated:

**LET MY PEOPLE GO → THAT THEY MAY SERVE ME**

The faculties are being transferred from one government to another.

### v17–18 — The Source of Egypt Is Struck

Thus saith the LORD:

> “In this thou shalt know that I am the LORD.”

And the rod shall strike the waters of the river.

And:

> **“they shall be turned to blood.”**

The fish shall die, the river shall stink, and the Egyptians shall loathe to drink its water.

The first judgment strikes the **life-source of Egypt**. The system of bondage depends upon this source for nourishment, fertility, commerce, and continuity; God now exposes it as incapable of giving true life.

**WATER → BLOOD**

What appeared to sustain the old kingdom becomes a sign of death.

The judgment begins at the source.

### v19–21 — The Judgment Spreads Through the Waters

And Aaron stretches out the rod over:

**streams,  
rivers,  
ponds,  
and pools of water.**

And there is blood throughout all the land of Egypt.

The judgment is not confined to one isolated place.

It spreads through the entire **water-system of the old order**.

And the fish die.

The river stinks.

The Egyptians cannot drink.

The source upon which the kingdom depended has become unusable.

The ruling consciousness is beginning to discover that what it treated as life cannot sustain it when exposed to **divine judgment**.

### v22 — The Magicians Reproduce the Judgment

And the magicians of Egypt did so with their enchantments.

They can imitate the sign, but what do they accomplish?

They make **more blood**.

They reproduce the condition of judgment. The counterfeit power can imitate manifestation, but it cannot **restore the water**, reverse what God has done, or provide life. Its power remains trapped inside the condition of Egypt.

The distinction is therefore becoming clearer:

**Divine authority transforms and ultimately delivers.**

**Counterfeit authority can imitate, but it cannot redeem.**

### v23 — Pharaoh Returns to His House

And Pharaoh turned and went into his house:

> “neither did he set his heart to this also.”

The ruling consciousness refuses to **set its heart** upon what has been revealed.

The Nile has changed, the fish have died, and the water cannot be drunk. Yet Pharaoh returns to his house. The external sign has not produced internal reorientation.

### v24–25 — Egypt Digs for Water

And all the Egyptians dig round about the river for water to drink:

> “for they could not drink of the water of the river.”

The kingdom that depended upon the Nile must now **dig around its own failed source** looking for something that can still sustain it.

And seven days are fulfilled after the LORD smote the river.

The first great confrontation has exposed a fundamental truth:

**the old order cannot supply the life it appeared to possess.**

Pharaoh still rules and Israel is still physically in Egypt, but the source sustaining the kingdom of bondage has been struck.

**The dismantling has begun.**

## Fractal Notes & References

Exodus 7 develops the recurring scriptural pattern in which **divine authority first exposes the insufficiency of the power that has been governing the field**. The serpent recalls Genesis 3, but here the serpent-power is taken up under divine command and Aaron's rod swallows the serpents of Egypt. The transformation of water into blood also forms a striking counterpart to the ministry of Jesus: Exodus begins its public signs with **water becoming blood under judgment**, while John records Jesus' first sign as **water becoming wine at a wedding feast** (John 2). One exposes the death hidden within the old order; the other manifests abundance within the new. The plague also anticipates Revelation, where waters again become blood under judgment. Within the immediate Exodus fractal, the progression is now clear: the faculties are enslaved, the deliverer is drawn out, the Presence is encountered, the commission is embodied, resistance intensifies, the promise is reaffirmed, and now **divine authority begins systematically dismantling the ruling order of bondage**.

## Onomastics & Name Meanings

| Name | Textual / Linguistic Meaning | BKV Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Moses — Mosheh** | Exodus 2:10 connects the name with *mashah*, **“to draw out”** | **The Drawn-Out One / delivering consciousness**, now standing in divine authority before the ruler of bondage |
| **Aaron — Aharon** | Etymology uncertain; Scripture does not self-define the name | Functionally **the faculty of expression**, giving outward voice and action to the commission carried by Moses |
| **Israel — Yisrael** | Genesis 32:28 associates the name with striving with God and prevailing | **God-Prevails**; the faculties claimed by God and being released from foreign government |
| **Egypt — Mitsrayim** | Historical name of Egypt | **The state of bondage / foreign inner government**, whose sustaining structures are now being exposed |
| **Pharaoh** | Egyptian royal title derived from the **“great house”** | **The ruling house of bondage**, the governing consciousness that refuses the higher authority of God |
| **Hebrew — ʿIvri** | Commonly associated with *ʿavar*, **to pass over / cross**, though the historical derivation remains debated | Suggestive of the faculties called to **cross out of the present order** toward service of God |

The most important naming movement in Exodus 7 actually began two chapters earlier.

Pharaoh asked:

> **“Who is the LORD?”**

God now says:

> **“In this thou shalt know that I am the LORD.”**

That question and answer organize the plague narrative.

The plagues are not merely punishments inflicted upon Egypt.

They progressively reveal **which power actually governs reality**.

Pharaoh claims the people.

Egypt claims their labor.

The Nile appears to supply their world.

The magicians appear able to reproduce divine power.

Exodus 7 begins stripping those claims away.

And underneath the entire confrontation remains the declaration established in Exodus 6:

> **“I am the LORD.”**
