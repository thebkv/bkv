# PSALM 73 — THE SANCTUARY REVEALS THE TRUTH

The psalmist knows that God is good, yet he nearly loses his faith when he sees the prosperity of the wicked. As can readily be seen in today's world, those who disregard God seem to enjoy wealth, strength, and freedom from trouble, while he struggles despite trying to keep his heart clean. He begins to wonder whether following God has been worth the cost. His mistake is to judge his life by outward appearances, mistaking temporary prosperity for lasting security. 

Everything changes when he enters the sanctuary of God. There he begins to understand where each path ultimately leads, and he sees that the prosperity he envied rests upon an unstable, and indeed, imaginary, foundation. The disciple must learn this same lesson. When he measures his life against the apparent success of others, envy and discouragement can cause him to lose his footing. 

But in the presence of God, his understanding is restored. He discovers that his greatest inheritance is not wealth, comfort, or freedom from difficulty, but God himself. What began as a struggle over why the wicked prosper ends with the psalmist declaring, "But it is good for me to draw near to God." (Psalm 73:28)

## The Savior Test

The psalmist begins by looking at the prosperity of the wicked and wondering whether keeping his heart clean has been worthwhile. Everything changes when he enters the sanctuary of God. He begins to see beyond their present circumstances to where their lives are leading.

Almost 1000 years later, the Lord gives the disciple the very instruction he needs:

> "Judge not according to the appearance, but judge righteous judgment." — John 7:24

This is the heart of Psalm 73. The psalmist nearly loses his footing because he judges by what he sees. The wicked appear strong, wealthy, and free from trouble, while the man seeking God experiences difficulty and correction. From the outside, it seems that the wrong people are prospering. But Jesus tells us that appearances are not sufficient grounds for judgment. The disciple must learn to see according to the truth of God.

Jesus then asks a question that exposes the weakness of worldly prosperity:

> "For what is a man profited, if he shall gain the whole world, and lose his own soul?" — Matthew 16:26 [The Rock and the Keys](matthew16.md)  

In Matthew 19 he says:
> And: "It is easier for a camel to go through the eye of a needle than for a rich man to enter the kingdom of God." - Also see Mark 10, Luke 18

This message from the Savior could not be more clear.

The psalmist had been measuring success by wealth, comfort, and outward security. Jesus reveals what was missing from that calculation. A man may possess everything the world considers desirable and still be losing the very life that matters most. The disciple must learn to consider where a path leads, rather than merely what it appears to offer today.

Jesus also describes two men who build their houses upon different foundations. One builds upon rock, the other upon sand. Both houses stand for a time, but when the storm comes, only the house founded upon the rock remains (Matthew 7:24–27).

This helps explain the slippery places of Psalm 73. What appeared secure to the psalmist was actually resting upon an unstable foundation. The prosperity he envied could not guarantee the lasting safety he imagined it possessed. He had almost abandoned the true foundation because he was distracted by the appearance of another man's house.

But the psalm does not end with a warning against wealth. It leads the disciple to something much greater.

> "Whom have I in heaven but thee? and there is none upon earth that I desire beside thee." — Psalm 73:25

Jesus expresses this same priority:

> "But seek ye first the kingdom of God, and his righteousness; and all these things shall be added unto you." — Matthew 6:33

The disciple who seeks the kingdom first is learning to desire what the psalmist finally discovers in the sanctuary. He no longer has to measure the goodness of God by comparing his circumstances with those of other men. He can trust the Father while continuing to seek the life and righteousness of His kingdom.

The Savior Test reveals the movement of the whole psalm. The Lord calls the disciple away from judging by appearances, shows him the danger of gaining the world at the expense of his soul, and teaches him to build upon what will endure.

The disciple should ask himself whether he has been envying something that only looks desirable from a distance. Has he mistaken outward prosperity for the blessing of God? Has he allowed comparison to make him doubt the value of the life Christ is teaching him to live?

The psalmist found his answer when he entered the sanctuary. Jesus brings the disciple to that same discovery: the greatest treasure is found in knowing the Father and living in His kingdom.

The psalmist finally says:

> "But it is good for me to draw near to God." — Psalm 73:28

This is where his nearly slipping feet find their footing again.


# Interactive KJV

## Psalm 73

**1** Truly God is good to [Israel](#israel), even to such as are of a [clean heart](#clean-heart).

> **BKV:** God is always good to the prevailing disciple whose inner life is undivided toward Him.

**2** But as for me, my [feet](#feet) were almost gone; my [steps](#steps) had well nigh slipped.

> **BKV:** My daily walk nearly departed from the path because my thinking began to drift from truth.

**3** For I was envious at the [foolish](#foolish), when I saw the prosperity of the [wicked](#wicked).

> **BKV:** I compared my journey with those living apart from God and mistook temporary appearance for lasting reality.

**4** For there are no [bands](#bands) in their death: but their [strength](#strength) is firm.

> **BKV:** They seem free from restraint, and their natural strength appears secure.

**5** They are not in [trouble](#trouble) as other men; neither are they plagued like other men.

> **BKV:** Their outward life seems unusually free from difficulty.

**6** Therefore [pride](#pride) compasseth them about as a [chain](#chain); [violence](#violence) covereth them as a [garment](#garment).

> **BKV:** Self-exaltation becomes their ornament, and destructive ways become part of their identity.

**7** Their [eyes](#eyes) stand out with fatness: they have more than [heart](#heart) could wish.

> **BKV:** Their perception is distorted by abundance, and their desires seem endlessly satisfied.

**8** They are corrupt, and speak wickedly concerning oppression: they speak loftily.

> **BKV:** Their thinking becomes elevated above humility, and they justify selfishness with confidence.
