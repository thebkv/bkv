# JOEL 2

## 02 — REND YOUR HEART: THE SPIRIT POURED OUT UPON ALL FLESH

[image prompt: A gathering outside the temple in ancient Jerusalem, with a watchman holding a ram’s horn and people of different ages standing in prayer. A break in the clouds sends warm light across the stone court. Biblical oil painting, historically grounded clothing, no lettering.]

## The Natural Reading

> "If any man thirst, let him come unto me, and drink. He that believeth on me, as the scripture hath said, out of his belly shall flow rivers of living water." — John 7:37–38

The trumpet sounds on Zion. Joel describes the day of the LORD as darkness spreading over the mountains. An advancing host leaves behind the opposite of what it found. Before it the land is like Eden, and behind it lies a wilderness. Joel compares the rush to horses and the noise to chariots and fire. The ranks hold as they climb walls, cross the city, and come through windows. The very lights of heaven fail, and the LORD gives voice before his army. Joel asks who can endure such a day.

At that point the LORD speaks to the people about what they can do now. They can turn to him with all their heart, fasting, weeping, and mourning. To rend a garment is a visible act of grief. Joel asks for the grief to reach the heart. The invitation rests on the Lord's character. He is gracious and merciful, slow to anger, and abundant in kindness. Joel leaves the outcome in the Lord's hands as he asks whether God may turn and leave a blessing behind him, enough to bring offerings to his house again.

The trumpet sounds a second time, calling the people together. Elders, children, and nursing infants are all included. The bride and bridegroom leave their rooms. The priests weep between the porch and the altar, asking the Lord to spare his own people and keep them from becoming a reproach. Their prayer takes up the question the nations might ask when they see Judah brought low. Where is their God?

The Lord answers with pity for his people and zeal for his land. He promises grain, wine, and oil, and says he will drive away the northern host. The summons to fear gives way to words addressed to the land, the beasts, and the children of Zion. Pastures grow again, the vine and fig tree yield fruit, and rain fills the threshing floors and vats with the produce of a new season. The Lord promises to restore the years the locust has eaten. The people will eat and praise him, and they will know that he is in the midst of Israel.

Then Joel says what the Lord will do afterward. He will pour out his Spirit on all flesh. Sons and daughters will prophesy. The old will dream and the young will see visions. Even servants and handmaids are named. The Lord also speaks of wonders in heaven and earth before his great and terrible day. The chapter brings that warning beside a promise. Whoever calls on the name of the LORD will be delivered, and there will be deliverance in Zion among the remnant whom the Lord calls.

The disciple can stop at the second trumpet and consider what he would bring to that assembly. The Lord asks for the heart itself. The promises that follow give the disciple reason to entrust it to him. Joel shows the Lord providing food for his people, making his presence known among them, and giving his own Spirit.

## The Savior Test

> "And it shall come to pass afterward, that I will pour out my spirit upon all flesh; and your sons and your daughters shall prophesy, your old men shall dream dreams, your young men shall see visions:" — Joel 2:28

> "If any man thirst, let him come unto me, and drink. He that believeth on me, as the scripture hath said, out of his belly shall flow rivers of living water." — John 7:37–38

Joel's promise reaches people across the whole household. Jesus stands in the feast and calls anyone who thirsts to come to him. John explains that the living water is the Spirit whom believers would receive, and he connects the giving of that Spirit to Jesus being glorified (John 7:39).

Peter makes the connection publicly at Pentecost. When the crowd asks what is happening, Peter answers that this is what Joel spoke about (Acts 2:16–21). He then says that Jesus, exalted at the right hand of God, received the promise of the Holy Ghost from the Father and poured out what the crowd saw and heard (Acts 2:33). Peter names both the ancient promise and the one through whom the gift has come.

The disciple can now hear the two invitations together. Joel calls the people to return with their whole heart. Jesus calls the thirsty to come to him and drink. The Lord who can fill an empty threshing floor can also give what the heart has no means of producing. Jesus says that the water will flow from within the one who believes. The Spirit received from him becomes a source of life that reaches beyond the disciple's own need.
