# LEVITICUS 2

## THE GIFT OF THE HARVEST

*Bringing the work of your hands to God*

## BKV Insight

**Leviticus 2 is about bringing your ordinary work into your life with God.**

The first chapter brought us to the altar with an animal from the herd or flock.

Now someone comes with flour.

That changes the picture.

This grain came from a field. Someone planted it, waited for it, harvested it, ground it, and prepared it. In some cases the offering is already baked or cooked.

God is receiving something that has passed through human hands.

That is important for the disciple.

Most of your life is made of ordinary things. You work. You make things. You solve problems. You prepare food. You earn money. You care for people. You take what God has given you and do something with it.

Leviticus 2 says that this part of your life belongs with God too.

The worshipper brings some of what his field has produced. Part of it is placed on the altar, and the rest becomes food for the priests.

So what God has provided becomes an offering, and the offering becomes provision for someone else.

That is a beautiful picture of work under God.

You receive something from Him. You work with it. Then what grows through your life becomes something you can give.

### Know

Your ordinary work can belong to God.

Leviticus 2 does not separate worship from the field, the harvest, the flour, or the kitchen. These ordinary things are brought right to His dwelling.

God is interested in what your life produces.

### Notice

Notice what is growing through the life God has given you.

Maybe it is a skill you have developed, work you are able to do, money you have earned, something you know how to make, or simply the ability to help someone who needs what you have.

The question is what happens to the harvest once it reaches your hands.

### Ask Yourself

What has God allowed to grow through my life?

How can I bring some of that back into His service?

## Canonical History & Context

Leviticus 2 continues God's instructions about offerings at the Tabernacle.

This time the offering comes from the harvest.

The KJV calls it a “meat offering,” but the offering described here is grain. The Hebrew word is **minḥah**, meaning a gift, tribute, or offering.

A person could bring fine flour with oil and frankincense. The offering could also be baked in an oven, cooked on a flat plate, or prepared in a pan. At the end of the chapter, God gives instructions for bringing the first grain of the harvest.

The priest takes part of the offering and burns it on the altar. The rest belongs to Aaron and his sons for food.

God also gives instructions about how the offering is prepared. Leaven and honey are not burned on the altar in this offering, while salt is required. God calls it “the salt of the covenant.”

The chapter is very practical.

Israel is learning that the food produced by the land can be brought into their worship of the God who gave them the land.

## Natural Reading

### Verses 1–3

The first worshipper comes with fine flour mixed with oil and frankincense.

The priest takes a handful and burns it on the altar. The rest becomes food for Aaron and his sons.

The whole gift belongs to God, but God does not require all of it to disappear in the fire. Part of what is given to Him feeds the people serving at His dwelling.

**BKV:** This is what can happen to the fruit of a life lived with God.

What He gives you can pass through your hands and become useful to someone else.

Your work can feed more than yourself.

### Verses 4–10

The next offerings have been prepared into food.

Some are baked. Some are cooked on a flat plate. Some are prepared in a pan.

The grain has been worked.

That gives us another part of the picture.

God gave the earth and the seed, but the bread did not simply appear. Someone cultivated the field, harvested the grain, ground it, mixed it, and prepared it.

Then the finished work was brought to God.

**BKV:** God can receive what you have done with what He gave you.

A gift from God does not have to remain undeveloped. You can learn, practice, build, prepare, and become good at something.

Then you can ask what that developed ability is for.

It can become part of your service to God.

### Verses 11–13

God now gives instructions about the ingredients.

Leaven and honey are not burned on the altar in this offering. Salt must be included.

The text calls it:

> “the salt of the covenant of thy God.”

The chapter does not tell us to turn every ingredient into a separate symbol. The clear point about the salt is the one Scripture gives us: covenant.

This gift is being offered inside a relationship.

**BKV:** The disciple's work with God is also part of a relationship.

You are learning to live with Him, receive from Him, and give from what you have received.

### Verses 14–16

Finally, God speaks about the first grain of the harvest.

Israel does not have to wait until everything has been gathered, stored, eaten, and sold before remembering God.

The beginning can be brought to Him.

**BKV:** This changes the way the disciple receives good things.

When something begins to bear fruit in your life, remember where it came from.

Bring God into the harvest while it is happening.

## Fractal Pattern

The main pattern of Leviticus 2 is simple:

**God gives, His people receive, and what they receive becomes something they can offer.**

We see this again when Israel enters the Promised Land. In Deuteronomy 26, the people bring the first fruit of the land before God and remember that He is the One who brought them out of Egypt and gave them the land.

Proverbs says:

> “Honour the LORD with thy substance, and with the firstfruits of all thine increase.”
>
> — Proverbs 3:9

Much later, the Philippians send material help to Paul. Paul describes their gift as:

> “an odour of a sweet smell, a sacrifice acceptable, wellpleasing to God.”
>
> — Philippians 4:18

The same basic movement remains.

Something has been placed in your hands.

What will you do with it?

## Meaning of Names

| Word | Meaning | Why It Matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Minḥah** | Gift, tribute, offering | The grain offering is first of all something brought and presented to God. |
| **Memorial portion** | The part burned on the altar | Part of the gift is offered on the altar while the remainder supports the priests. |
| **Salt of the covenant** | Salt required with the offering | Scripture itself connects the salt with Israel's covenant relationship with God. |
| **First produce** | The beginning of the harvest | Israel acknowledges God while the harvest is arriving, rather than only after everything has been received. |

---

Leviticus 2 takes something completely ordinary and brings it into the Presence of God.

Flour.

Bread.

A harvest.

The work of someone's hands.

For the disciple, that makes the chapter surprisingly close to everyday life.

God has already placed things in your hands.

The question is what they will become there.
