# Exodus 19 — The Delivered People Are Prepared to Meet God

## BKV Insight

Exodus 19 reveals where the Exodus has been heading all along: **God brought His people out of Egypt to bring them unto Himself.** Israel has been delivered from Pharaoh, sustained through the wilderness, defended from attack, and organized as a people. Now the LORD brings them to Sinai and prepares them to hear His voice.

God tells Israel what He has done and what He is calling them to become. He carried them out of Egypt, brought them to Himself, and now calls them His treasured possession, **a kingdom of priests and a holy nation**. The people wash their clothes, remain within the bounds God sets around the mountain, and make themselves ready.

For the disciple, the movement is clear. **God does not free the life merely to leave it to itself. He brings it out from the rule of the old man so that the whole person can belong to Him, come under His order, and learn to hear and obey His voice.**

## Canonical History & Context

Exodus 19 brings Israel to Mount Sinai in the third month after leaving Egypt and begins the great Sinai section of Exodus. The people camp before the mountain where Moses had first encountered God at the burning bush. The LORD had already told Moses that after he brought Israel out of Egypt, the people would serve God upon this mountain (Exodus 3:12). Now Moses has returned with the people.

Moses ascends the mountain, and the LORD first reminds Israel of His deliverance from Egypt. He then declares the covenant calling before them: if they obey His voice and keep His covenant, they will be His treasured possession, a kingdom of priests, and a holy nation.

The people agree to obey. The LORD commands them to prepare for three days through sanctification, washing, and strict boundaries around the mountain. On the third day He descends upon Sinai in fire amid thunder, lightning, thick cloud, smoke, earthquake, and the sound of a trumpet. Moses brings the people out of the camp to meet God. Exodus 20 will begin with God speaking His commandments.

## Natural Reading

### v1–4 — “I Brought You Unto Myself”

In the third month after Israel had:

> “gone forth out of the land of Egypt”

they come into the wilderness of Sinai and camp:

> “before the mount.”

The people who once stood under Pharaoh's authority now stand before the mountain of God.

Moses goes up unto God, and the LORD calls to him from the mountain:

> “Thus shalt thou say to the house of Jacob, and tell the children of Israel.”

God begins with what Israel has already seen:

> “Ye have seen what I did unto the Egyptians.”

Then He tells them what He Himself has done for them:

> **“I bare you on eagles' wings, and brought you unto myself.”**

This sentence gives the Exodus its direction.

God did not simply bring Israel **out of Egypt**.

He brought Israel **unto Himself**.

For the disciple, this gives deliverance its purpose. The parts of life that once served another ruler are being brought under the rule of God. **Freedom from bondage becomes belonging to the LORD.**

### v5–6 — A Peculiar Treasure, a Kingdom of Priests

Now the LORD tells Israel what life with Him will require:

> “if ye will obey my voice indeed, and keep my covenant”

then:

> “ye shall be a peculiar treasure unto me above all people: for all the earth is mine.”

The Hebrew *segullah* speaks of a treasured possession, something particularly belonging to the king.

The whole earth belongs to God, yet Israel is being set apart as His covenant people.

Then comes the fuller calling:

> **“ye shall be unto me a kingdom of priests, and an holy nation.”**

The people who had spent their strength building for Pharaoh are now being called to serve God.

They are to hear His voice.

They are to keep His covenant.

They are to become a people set apart for Him.

The change from Egypt is more than a change of location. **Israel is passing from service under Pharaoh into a life that belongs to God.**

Within the disciple, the same movement takes place. The powers of the person that were once used in service of fear, appetite, pride, habit, or the demands of the old life are being consecrated to God.

### v7–9 — The People Agree to Hear God

Moses calls the elders and lays before them:

> “all these words which the LORD commanded him.”

Then:

> “all the people answered together”

and said:

> **“All that the LORD hath spoken we will do.”**

The people answer as one body.

Moses brings their answer back to the LORD.

God then tells Moses:

> “Lo, I come unto thee in a thick cloud”

so that:

> **“the people may hear when I speak with thee.”**

Israel already knows something of God through what He has done. They have seen Egypt judged, passed through the sea, eaten the bread He provided, drunk water from the rock, and seen victory over Amalek.

Now God is preparing them to **hear Him speak**.

For the disciple, this is another stage of formation. We may first recognize God through what He does in our lives. But the life being formed under Him must also learn to hear His Word and obey it.

### v10–15 — Sanctify the People

The LORD tells Moses:

> **“Go unto the people, and sanctify them to day and to morrow.”**

They are to:

> “wash their clothes”

and:

> **“be ready against the third day.”**

The preparation is concrete.

They wash.

They wait.

They prepare themselves.

God also commands Moses:

> **“set bounds unto the people round about.”**

The people must not climb the mountain or touch its border.

The God who says **“I brought you unto myself”** is the same God who tells them how they are to approach.

Israel is being brought near, but they are not free to define that nearness for themselves.

**God sets the terms of approach.**

For the disciple, consecration carries the same lesson. Coming to God involves the whole life. What we do, how we prepare ourselves, what we obey, and what we refuse all come under His direction. Nearness to God does not erase holiness. It teaches us to live within it.

### v16–17 — The People Come Out to Meet God

On the third day:

> “there were thunders and lightnings, and a thick cloud upon the mount”

and:

> “the voice of the trumpet exceeding loud.”

The whole camp trembles.

Then Moses:

> **“brought forth the people out of the camp to meet with God.”**

Israel has been brought out once again.

They were brought **out of Egypt**.

Now Moses brings them **out of the camp to meet with God**.

The purpose announced in verse 4 is becoming visible in the scene itself:

> **“I brought you unto myself.”**

The people who once answered Pharaoh's commands now stand before the LORD, waiting to hear His voice.

For the disciple, this is the direction of the whole movement out of bondage. God draws the life away from what ruled it so that the person can stand before Him.

### v18–20 — The LORD Descends and Moses Ascends

> “And mount Sinai was altogether on a smoke, because the LORD descended upon it in fire.”

The smoke rises:

> “as the smoke of a furnace”

and:

> “the whole mount quaked greatly.”

The scene is physical and overwhelming.

Fire.

Smoke.

A trembling mountain.

A trumpet growing louder and louder.

Then:

> “Moses spake, and God answered him by a voice.”

The LORD comes down upon Sinai:

> **“and the LORD called Moses up to the top of the mount.”**

The movement is simple:

**the LORD descends  
→ Moses is called upward  
→ God speaks.**

Moses now stands between the LORD upon the mountain and the people below. He receives God's words and carries them to Israel.

The chapter does not leave God's Presence as an abstract idea. **God comes down. Moses is called up. The people stand below. God speaks.**

### v21–25 — The Bounds Remain

Even now the LORD sends Moses back down to warn the people:

> “lest they break through unto the LORD to gaze.”

The priests must also:

> “sanctify themselves.”

Moses points out that bounds have already been established around the mountain. The LORD nevertheless sends him down again and tells him:

> “thou shalt come up, thou, and Aaron with thee”

while the priests and the people remain in their appointed places.

Everyone belongs to the same covenant people, but everyone does not have the same function in this moment.

The pattern will become more developed later in Exodus through the priesthood and the Tabernacle. Here its basic form is already visible:

**God is present.  
The people are gathered to Him.  
The people are consecrated.  
God appoints the way they approach.  
God speaks.**

Moses goes down to the people.

Everything is ready.

The next chapter begins:

> **“And God spake all these words...”**

Israel has been brought out of Egypt.

Israel has been brought unto God.

Now Israel will hear His Word.

## Fractal Notes & References

Exodus 19 gives the purpose of the Exodus in the LORD's own words:

> **“I brought you unto myself.”**

That movement was announced at the burning bush. The LORD told Moses that after bringing the people out of Egypt:

> “ye shall serve God upon this mountain.”  
> — Exodus 3:12

Moses first came to the mountain alone. Now he returns with the people God sent him to bring out.

The movement continues through the rest of Exodus. At Sinai, God descends and Israel gathers before Him. Later the Tabernacle will place His dwelling in the midst of the camp. Exodus 29:46 states the purpose plainly:

> “I brought them forth out of the land of Egypt, that I may dwell among them.”

The larger movement is:

**brought out of Egypt  
→ brought unto God  
→ consecrated to God  
→ taught by God  
→ God dwelling among them.**

The New Testament directly takes up the language of Exodus 19. First Peter 2:9 describes those gathered to Christ as “a royal priesthood, an holy nation, a peculiar people” and joins that calling to being brought out of darkness into God's marvellous light. Revelation 1:5–6 likewise joins redemption through the blood of Jesus Christ with being made kings and priests unto God.

These are strong canonical connections because the New Testament itself reuses the language of Sinai for the people redeemed through Christ.

For the disciple, Exodus 19 therefore gives a basic pattern of spiritual formation:

**God brings us out so that He may bring us to Himself.**

Deliverance removes the old ruler. Consecration gives the delivered life a new direction. The faculties that once served the old life are gathered under God, prepared to hear Him, and increasingly ordered around His Presence.

## Onomastics & Key Terms

| Name / Term | Textual / Linguistic Meaning | Minimum Functional Role / BKV Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Moses — Mosheh** | Exodus 2:10 connects the name with *mashah*, “to draw out” | **The Drawn-Out One**, now bringing the people drawn out of Egypt to meet with God |
| **Israel — Yisrael** | Genesis 32:28 connects the name with striving with God and prevailing | The delivered covenant people gathered before the LORD; in BKV, the faculties of the person being brought together under God |
| **Jacob — Ya'aqov** | Genesis 25:26 associates the name with the heel; Genesis 27:36 also makes a narrative wordplay around supplanting | The ancestral covenant name by which the LORD addresses the people He has brought to Himself |
| **Sinai — Sinai** | Ancient place-name; precise etymology uncertain | The mountain where Israel is gathered, consecrated, and prepared to hear the LORD |
| **Covenant — Berith** | Covenant, binding agreement, established relationship | The relationship in which Israel belongs to the LORD, hears His voice, and is called to obedience |
| **Peculiar Treasure — Segullah** | Treasured possession, valued property belonging particularly to a ruler | Israel's stated relationship to God: His treasured covenant people within a world that already belongs to Him |
| **Priest — Kohen** | Priest; one serving in sacred office | In “kingdom of priests,” part of Israel's calling to belong to and serve the LORD |
| **Holy — Qadosh** | Holy, set apart | Israel is being set apart unto the LORD for His purpose |
| **Sanctify — Qadash** | To set apart, consecrate, make holy | The preparation God requires before the people meet Him at Sinai |
| **Bounds — Gevul** | Border, boundary, limit | The actual boundary God establishes around Sinai; in the chapter it shows that God Himself governs the people's approach |

The chapter's clearest interpretive key is not hidden in an uncertain name or secondary symbol. The LORD states it directly:

> **“I brought you unto myself.”**

That sentence explains why Israel had to leave Egypt.

The people have reached the mountain.

They have washed.

They have remained within the bounds.

The LORD has descended.

Moses has brought them out:

> **“to meet with God.”**

Now God speaks.
