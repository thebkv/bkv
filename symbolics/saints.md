# Biblical Symbolics — Saints
Version: 2026-09-30

## Core Meaning

In scripture, **saints are those who are set apart to God and belong to His kingdom**.

The word does not primarily describe unusually impressive or spiritually accomplished people. Its basic idea is **holiness or consecration**: people separated from the ordinary rule of the world and brought under the government of God.

Symbolically, the saints therefore represent **what in the disciple has been brought into alignment with God and now belongs to His order**.

When saints appear collectively, they can represent the growing body of thoughts, affections, desires, and faculties that have been reclaimed from the old government and brought under the rule of God.

## Narrative Functions of Saints

Saints frequently appear when scripture is distinguishing **what belongs to God's kingdom from what belongs to another government**.

Common patterns include:

• **Calling the saints** — setting apart what will belong to God  
• **Gathering the saints** — bringing what belongs to God into unity  
• **Opposing the saints** — the old or contrary government resisting divine order  
• **Overcoming the saints** — a temporary period in which the contrary power appears dominant  
• **The perseverance of the saints** — remaining aligned with God while under pressure  
• **The saints receiving the kingdom** — God's government becoming established in those who belong to Him  
• **The saints reigning** — what has been brought under God participating in His order and authority  

Saint imagery therefore frequently appears in passages concerned with **belonging, holiness, conflict, perseverance, inheritance, and government**.

## Observational Notes

Several structural patterns appear repeatedly:

• Saints are defined first by **belonging to God**, not by extraordinary personal achievement  
• The saints frequently appear in conflict with powers representing **another kind of government**  
• Daniel 7 closely connects the saints with the **Son of Man and the receiving of the kingdom**  
• The saints can be opposed and temporarily overcome without the opposing power receiving the final kingdom  
• In the New Testament, ordinary believers can be addressed as saints because they have been **called and set apart in Christ**  
• The movement of the saints often progresses from **calling and separation to perseverance, inheritance, and government**

For the disciple, the saints can therefore represent **everything within the person that has been set apart from the old rule and brought into allegiance with God**. As that work spreads through the person, more of the inner life becomes part of the kingdom.
