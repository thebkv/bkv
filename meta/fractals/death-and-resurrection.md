# Death and Resurrection

## The Pattern

One of the deepest movements in Scripture is also one of the simplest:

**Something dies. God brings life out of it.**

A seed goes into the ground.

Israel goes down into Egypt.

Israel passes through the sea.

Jonah goes down into the deep.

A sacrifice is placed on the altar.

Jesus goes into the tomb.

Again and again, the way forward passes through an ending.

For the disciple, this means more than believing that Jesus rose from the dead or that we will rise after physical death.

Jesus describes a life in which the same pattern begins happening now.

Something in us must come to an end so that the life of Christ can appear more fully.

**Death → Burial → Resurrection → New Life**

---

## The Seed

Jesus gives one of the clearest pictures of the pattern in a seed.

> "Except a corn of wheat fall into the ground and die, it abideth alone: but if it die, it bringeth forth much fruit."
>
> — John 12:24

The seed does not remain as it was and also become the plant.

It goes into the ground.

Its old form is lost.

Then something larger comes out of what was buried.

This is important for the disciple because we often want new life without losing the old one.

We want resurrection without death.

But the seed shows the order.

**The old form is surrendered before the new life appears.**

---

## Abraham and Isaac

Abraham receives Isaac as the promised son.

Then God tells Abraham to offer him.

The promise itself appears to be brought to an end.

Abraham takes Isaac up the mountain, binds him, and lays him on the altar.

Isaac does not physically die. God stops Abraham.

But Hebrews explains what Abraham believed:

> "Accounting that God was able to raise him up, even from the dead."
>
> — Hebrews 11:19

Abraham had to surrender even the thing God had given him.

He could not possess the promise apart from the God who gave it.

The movement is:

**Promise → surrender → apparent loss → received back from God**

Sometimes what must die is not an obviously bad thing.

Sometimes it is our possession of a good thing.

---

## Joseph Goes Down

Joseph's life repeatedly moves downward before it moves upward.

He goes into the pit.

Then into slavery.

Then into prison.

Everything that seemed to belong to his former life is stripped away.

But the descent is not the end of his story.

Joseph is brought out of prison and raised into authority.

The one who went down becomes the one through whom many people are kept alive.

The pattern is:

**Descent → loss → hiddenness → raising → life for others**

Joseph's story shows something important about biblical resurrection.

Being brought up is not merely a return to where you were before.

Joseph does not return to his old life as Jacob's favored son.

He comes out carrying something he did not have when he went in.

---

## Israel Goes Down and Comes Up

The same movement happens to an entire people.

Israel goes down into Egypt.

At first Egypt preserves the family during famine.

Eventually it becomes the place of bondage.

Then God brings Israel out.

But leaving Egypt requires a passage through death.

At Passover, death passes through the land.

Then Israel comes to the sea.

Behind them is Egypt.

Before them is water.

There is no ordinary way forward.

They pass through the sea and emerge on the other side.

Paul later connects this passage with baptism:

> "And were all baptized unto Moses in the cloud and in the sea."
>
> — 1 Corinthians 10:2

Egypt is behind them.

A new life has begun.

But they cannot carry Egypt with them into the promised land.

The old life must be left behind.

---

## Jonah Goes Down

Jonah refuses God's call and flees.

Then the story keeps moving downward.

He goes down to Joppa.

Down into the ship.

Down into sleep.

Down into the sea.

Then into the fish.

Jonah finally describes his condition this way:

> "Out of the belly of hell cried I."
>
> — Jonah 2:2

He has reached the end of his flight.

Then God brings him up.

Jonah is returned to dry land, and the word of the Lord comes to him again:

> "Arise, go unto Nineveh."
>
> — Jonah 3:2

The man who would not arise had to go down before he could arise again.

**Call → resistance → descent → deathlike confinement → brought up → call again**

The second call sounds much like the first.

Jonah is the one who has changed.

---

## Jesus

All of these movements come into focus in Jesus.

He tells His disciples plainly that He will be killed and rise again.

Then He actually passes through death.

He is crucified.

He is buried.

He rises.

But Jesus also teaches His disciples that His death and resurrection establish the pattern of their lives.

He says:

> "If any man will come after me, let him deny himself, and take up his cross, and follow me."
>
> — Matthew 16:24

The cross is therefore not only something Jesus experiences while His disciples watch.

They follow Him into it.

Paul says the same thing another way:

> "Therefore we are buried with him by baptism into death."
>
> — Romans 6:4

And then:

> "Even so we also should walk in newness of life."
>
> — Romans 6:4

That is the whole movement.

**Death → burial → newness of life**

---

## Where This Happens in the Disciple

There are things in us that cannot be carried forward.

An old identity.

A ruling desire.

The need to control.

The need to be seen.

A way of protecting ourselves.

An attachment we will not surrender.

A life built around ourselves.

The natural instinct is to preserve these things while asking God to give us a better life.

But Jesus describes something more radical.

> "For whosoever will save his life shall lose it: but whosoever will lose his life for my sake, the same shall save it."
>
> — Luke 9:24

That is death and resurrection in the disciple.

You stop trying to preserve the life that must end.

You entrust it to God.

And what comes back is not simply the old life repaired.

It is life under a new government.

This can happen many times in the disciple's life.

A particular thing is brought to the cross.

It dies.

For a time, there may be only emptiness.

Then something new begins to live where the old thing ruled.

---

## The Fractal

**LIFE AS IT IS**

Something exists in its present form.

↓

**SURRENDER**

It is given up rather than preserved at all costs.

↓

**DEATH**

The old form reaches its end.

↓

**BURIAL / HIDDENNESS**

Nothing new may yet be visible.

↓

**RESURRECTION**

God brings forth what could not be produced by preserving the old life.

↓

**NEW LIFE**

What emerges does not simply return to the old condition. It lives differently.

This pattern can happen to a seed.

It can happen to a person.

It can happen to a nation.

It happens completely in Jesus Christ.

And it happens within the disciple who follows Him.

The scale changes.

The movement remains.

---

## Do Not Skip the Middle

One of the easiest ways to misunderstand this pattern is to jump directly from trouble to victory.

Scripture does not do that.

There is a tomb between the cross and resurrection.

There is a sea between Egypt and freedom.

There is a fish between Jonah's flight and his second call.

There is a prison between Joseph's slavery and his rule.

There is ground around the buried seed.

The middle matters.

Sometimes the disciple has surrendered something, but the new thing has not appeared yet.

That does not necessarily mean nothing is happening.

There is a part of this pattern where the old thing is gone and the new thing is still hidden.

Scripture calls us to remain faithful there too.

---

## The Savior Test

Jesus does not merely teach this pattern.

He lives it.

And then He says:

> "If any man will come after me, let him deny himself, and take up his cross, and follow me."
>
> — Matthew 16:24

The disciple follows the same movement.

Not by seeking suffering for its own sake.

Not by trying to destroy himself.

But by refusing to preserve the old life when following Christ requires surrendering it.

Jesus gives the promise on the other side:

> "Whosoever will lose his life for my sake, the same shall save it."
>
> — Luke 9:24

That is the pattern.

**What you keep trying to save cannot become the new thing God is bringing forth.**

The question for the disciple is:

**What am I trying to keep alive that God is asking me to surrender?**
