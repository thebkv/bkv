# Ahaziah

- **Corpus:** MBD
- **Type:** mbd_name
- **Language:** Heb
- **Pronunciation:** a-ha-zi'-ah

## Biblical / Dictionary Context

a King of Israel and son of Ahab and Jezebel (I Kings 22:40). b A king of Judah, son of Joram 
and Athaliah. (Athaliah was a daughter of Ahab and Jezebel. See II Kings 8:24 - 9:28.)

## Metaphysical

Although both these kings were idolatrous, and were directed in their reigns by the 
selfishness of the sense man, yet they represent the continuity of the inner spiritual will of man (whom 
Jehovah sustains, Jah holds, possesses), which selfishness cannot kill out.

In the case of Ahaziah, king of Judah, this idea is carried out quite clearly by the saving (hiding) 
of his son Joash, or Jehoash, from being destroyed by Athaliah. Later this son was given the throne, and 
he proved to be a good king. (See II Kings 11:1-17.)

Ahban, ah'-ban ( Heb.)- -brother of intelligence; brother of understanding, i. e., wise, discreet.

Son of Abishur by Abihail; a Judahite (I Chron. 2:29).

Metaphysical. The uniting (in consciousness) of strength and might to intelligence, or wisdom 
(brother of intelligence, wise, discreet). Abishur, father of Ahban, symbolizes a consciousness of 
strength brought about by uprightness and praise (Judah) while Abihail, mother of Ahban, signifies the 
idea that power and strength come from God and are spiritual qualities. When strength and power are 
unified with and express through wisdom, true wisdom being one with love, the individual grows in 
discretion and in strength of character; thus he is enabled more perfectly to avoid error and discern and 
express the good.

Aher, a'-her (Heb.) after; behind; following.

A Benjamite (I Chron. 7:12). He is called Aharah in I Chronicles 8:1, and Ahiram in Numbers

26:38.

Metaphysical. See AHARAH.

Ahi, a'-hi (Heb.) my brother; my friend; brotherly.

a A chief of the tribe of Asher (I Chron. 7:34). b A man of the tribe of Gad (I Chron. 5:15).

Metaphysical. An established thought of the brotherhood of man. This thought springs from the 
understanding that God is the one Father and that all men are brothers (Any brother, my friend, 
brotherly)
