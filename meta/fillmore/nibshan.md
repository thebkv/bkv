# Nibshan

- **Corpus:** MBD
- **Type:** mbd_name
- **Language:** Heb
- **Pronunciation:** nib'-shan

## Biblical / Dictionary Context

A city in the wilderness of Judah (Josh. 15:62).

## Metaphysical

A group of thoughts, fertile, productive, capable of bringing forth much good, 
belonging to the wilderness or uncultivated and undisciplined phase of man's consciousness. This group 
of thoughts has come under the jurisdiction of the prayer-and-praise faculty (Judah) for further 
adjustment and upliftment. Heated, glowing, furnace, suggest the purifying light, life, and love of Spirit. 
(See Isaiah 33:14, latter half of verse, to 17.) "The refining pot is for silver, and the furnace for gold; and 
a man IS tried by his praise" (margin, by "that which he praiseth, or, that whereof he boasteth"; Prov. 
27:21). In Jeremiah 1 1:4 the land of Egypt, out of which the Children of Israel were brought, is likened 
to "the iron furnace."

Nicanor, ni-ca'-n™r (Gk overcomer; victor; victorious; conqueror; steadfast; constant; 
courageous.

One of the seven men chosen by the Christians at Jerusalem to look after the distribution of 
provisions among the widows and others of the assembly there (Acts 6:5).

Metaphysical. An inner realization of victory, of overcoming, especially as relating to substance 
and supply for the outer or intellectual phase of man's consciousness that is at this time partially 
established

in Truth. (Grecian widows symbolize half truths, of a somewhat negative character, pertaining to the 
intellect. They seem to have been neglected by the apostles, or spiritual faculties of mind, who were 
giving their greatest attention to the Hebrews— the higher thoughts of the inner consciousness that were 
consecrated to Truth). The central idea in Nicanor is that of overcoming victory through steadfastness; 
in conjunction with this see Luke 8:15 and 21:19, with marginal notes.
