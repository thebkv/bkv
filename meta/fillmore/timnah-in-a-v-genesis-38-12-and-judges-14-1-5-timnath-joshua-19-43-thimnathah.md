# Timnah (in A. V., Genesis 38:12 and Judges 14:1-5, Timnath; Joshua, 19:43, Thimnathah)

- **Corpus:** MBD
- **Type:** mbd_name
- **Language:** Heb
- **Pronunciation:** Genesis 38:12 and Judges 14:1-5, Timnath; Joshua, 19:43, Thimnathah), tim- 
nah

## Meanings

- part
- portion
- number
- lot
- allotment
- assignment

## Biblical / Dictionary Context

a A Canaanitish city. Judah went up to Timnah to his sheep shearers (Gen. 38: 12). b A city of 
Judah (Josh. 15:10, 57). c A city of Dan (Josh. 19:43); however, this city evidently remained in the 
hands of the Philistines, for some years at least; it was the home of the Philistine woman whom Samson 
loved and desired to marry (Judg. 14:1, 2).

## Metaphysical

The error race belief that a share of the life forces in man rightly belongs to the 
use of the purely animal, physical, and sense phase of his being. (Timnah means portion, allotment; it 
was a Canaanitish city, where Judah kept his sheep. Sheep refer to the pure, natural life of the organism, 
and Canaanites represent the elemental life forces in man.)

While two of the cities by this name are mentioned as belonging to the Israelites (the tribes of 
Judah and Dan), the one in Dan (judgment) remained for a long time at least in possession of the 
Philistines (the outer senses). In Judges 14:5, the "vineyards of Timnah" are mentioned. Vineyards and 
grapes refer to life. The Philistine women to whom Samson attached himself, and who were the cause of 
his downfall, signify sensuality.
