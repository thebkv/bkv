# 1 SAMUEL 28

## 28 — THE KING AT THE DOOR OF ENDOR
### *The Silence of God and the Desperation of the Will*  
  
> *“And when Saul enquired of the LORD, the LORD answered him not, neither by dreams, nor by Urim, nor by prophets.”*  
  
> 
> — **1 Samuel 28 : 6 KJV**  

⸻  
## BKV-NR — Narrative Reading  
  
### I. The Silence of Heaven (vv 1–6)  
  
The Philistines gathered for war. David was dwelling in Ziklag, hidden;  
  
but Saul stood alone. He enquired of the Source, yet heard no answer—  
  
neither through dreams, nor Urim, nor prophets.  
  
*When the outer will has rejected light too often,*  
  
*the channels of revelation fall silent.*  
⸻  
### II. The Search for Forbidden Counsel (vv 7–11)  
  
Saul said to his servants, > “Seek me a woman that hath a familiar spirit.”  
  
They found one in **Endor (Spring of Generation).**  
  
He disguised himself and went by night.  
  
He said to the woman, > “Bring me up Samuel.”  
  
*The will, unable to rise to the Source, attempts to reach backward through the psychic realm—*  
  
*a forbidden reversal of spiritual order.*  
⸻  
### III. The Disturbance of the Past (vv 12–15)  
  
When the woman saw Samuel, she cried aloud,  
  
for she perceived Saul.  
  
And Samuel said, > “Why hast thou disquieted me?”  
  
Saul answered, > “I am sore distressed; the Philistines make war against me,  
  
and God is departed from me, and answereth me no more.”  
  
*False inquiry disturbs memory rather than receives revelation.*  
  
*The past cannot give what only the living Spirit can speak.*  
⸻  
### IV. The Prophecy of Inevitable End (vv 16–19)  
  
Samuel said, > “Wherefore then dost thou ask of me, seeing the LORD is departed from thee and is become thine enemy? …  
  
Tomorrow shalt thou and thy sons be with me.”  
  
Saul fell full length on the ground.  
  
*The word of truth, when summoned through fear, condemns rather than comforts.*  
  
*Judgment is not inflicted by God but drawn forth by disobedience.*  
⸻  
### V. The Kindness of the Witch (vv 20–25)  
  
The woman saw Saul trembling and bade him eat.  
  
She brought him bread and a calf, and he ate and departed that night.  
  
*Even in error, there remains a trace of mercy;*  
  
*compassion mingles with judgment as the will fades into darkness.*  
⸻  
## Commentary on the Action  
  
**1 Samuel 28** reveals the **Law of False Inquiry**—  
  
the futility of seeking spiritual answers through inverted means.  
  
- **Saul** represents the egoic will estranged from the Source, turning to substitute channels.  
- **Endor** is the psychic well of human imagination—spiritual power misdirected into the past.  
- **The Woman** is mediumistic sensitivity without illumination.  
- **Samuel** is the echo of truth, not its living voice.  
  
When the heart cannot endure silence, it turns to noise.  
  
But the silence it feared was itself the door to renewal.  
⸻

## Addendum — The Law of False Inquiry  
  
When the voice of Spirit is silent, wait.  
  
To seek answers through fear is to speak into an empty mirror.  
  
True guidance returns only to a heart at rest.  
  
Better the night of silence than the whisper of illusion.  
⸻  
## Core BKV Principle — 1 Samuel 28  
  
> **Silence is not abandonment but invitation;**  
  
> 
> **fear turns it into illusion.** 🕊️

## BKV-DR — Devotional Reading  
### *The Silence and the Shadow*  
⸻  
28 : 1–6  
  
The enemy rose, and I sought the Source,  
  
but heard nothing—no dream, no word, no sign.  
  
The heavens were still.  
  
My own breath echoed in the emptiness.  
  
I feared the silence and called it abandonment.  
⸻  
28 : 7–11  
  
Then I said within my fear, “Find me a voice.”  
  
And they brought me to a well of shadows.  
  
I disguised my need as strength and whispered, “Bring me Samuel.”  
  
But I summoned only memory, not revelation.  
⸻  
28 : 12–19  
  
The past rose and spoke, “Why disquiet me?”  
  
And I answered, “Because I am afraid.”  
  
The echo replied, “You knew the truth and left it.”  
  
So the light I had once known condemned my own departure.  
⸻  
28 : 20–25  
  
I fell to the ground, trembling.  
  
A woman of pity fed me bread.  
  
Even error offered mercy,  
  
for compassion lingers where judgment falls.  
  
And I went my way into the night,  
  
bearing the weight of unanswered prayer.  
⸻  
### Meditative Reflection  
  
> When God is silent, wait.  
  
> 
> To force an answer is to raise a shadow.  
  
> 
> Better the long quiet than the false voice,  
  
> 
> for truth will speak again when the heart is still.
