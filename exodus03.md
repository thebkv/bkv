# Exodus 3 — The Deliverer Turns Toward God

## BKV Insight

Exodus 3 describes the decisive **reorientation of the delivering consciousness toward God**. Moses has already been drawn out of Egypt, but separation alone cannot deliver Israel; at the burning bush he turns aside, removes his shoes, hears God call him by name, and learns that the work of deliverance proceeds from **I AM**, not from his own power. The disciple who tried to accomplish liberation by his own hand in Exodus 2 is now prepared to become the instrument through which God acts.

## Canonical History & Context

Moses is living in Midian after fleeing Pharaoh and is tending the flock of Jethro, his father-in-law, when he comes to Horeb, later identified as the mountain of God. There the angel of the LORD appears in a bush that burns without being consumed. God identifies Himself as the God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, declares that He has seen Israel's affliction and heard their cry, and commissions Moses to bring Israel out of Egypt. Moses questions both his own fitness and the identity by which he should present God to Israel. God answers, “I AM THAT I AM,” commissions Moses to speak to the elders and Pharaoh, foretells Pharaoh's resistance, and promises that Israel will leave Egypt with the wealth of the Egyptians.

## Natural Reading

### v1 — The Deliverer Comes to the Mountain of God

Now **Moses — the Drawn-Out One** kept the flock of **Jethro**, his father-in-law, the priest of **Midian — the place of separation and preparation**.

And he led the flock to the backside of the desert, and came to **Horeb — the mountain of God**.

The deliverer who once lived in Pharaoh's house is now a shepherd in the wilderness.

He has been:

**Drawn Out → Made a Stranger → Led into Separation**

And now, without yet knowing what awaits him, he arrives at **the mountain of God**.

### v2–3 — The Fire That Does Not Consume

And the angel of the LORD appeared unto him in a flame of **fire** out of the midst of a bush.

And Moses looked, and behold, the bush burned with fire, and the bush was **not consumed**.

And Moses said:

> “I will now turn aside, and see this great sight.”

The fire of God is present, yet what it inhabits is not destroyed.

This is **divine fire as Presence rather than annihilation**.

And Moses does something essential:

**he turns aside.**

The delivering consciousness that once looked upon Egypt's bondage now turns its attention toward **the manifestation of God**.

### v4 — God Calls the Deliverer by Name

And when the LORD saw that he **turned aside to see**, God called unto him out of the midst of the bush:

> “Moses, Moses.”

And he said:

> “Here am I.”

God's call follows Moses' turning.

The Drawn-Out One becomes consciously available to the divine Presence:

**Turn → Hear → Answer**

The deliverer is no longer initiating the action.

He is **responding**.

### v5–6 — Remove the Shoes

And God said:

> “Draw not nigh hither: put off thy shoes from off thy feet, for the place whereon thou standest is **holy ground**.”

The feet represent the walk by which the disciple moves through the world.

Before Moses receives his commission, the old covering of that walk is removed.

He stands before God **without the ordinary protection and identity with which he has walked until now**.

And God said:

> “I am the God of thy father, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob.”

The God who now speaks is the same God who has governed the entire movement of promise.

Moses hides his face.

The delivering consciousness has encountered **the Source from which its authority must come**.

### v7–9 — God Has Seen, Heard, and Known

And the LORD said:

> “I have surely seen the affliction of my people which are in Egypt, and have heard their cry.”

Exodus 2 ended:

**God heard.  
God remembered.  
God looked.  
God knew.**

Now God reveals to Moses what that knowing means:

> “I am come down to deliver them.”

This is the great shift.

Israel's deliverance does not originate in Moses' anger at injustice.

It originates in **God's seeing, God's hearing, God's knowing, and God's coming down**.

The true Deliverer has now identified Himself.

### v8 — Out of Egypt Into a Good Land

And I am come down to deliver them out of the hand of **Egypt — bondage**, and to bring them up out of that land unto a **good land and a large**, unto a land flowing with **milk and honey**.

Deliverance has both a **from** and a **toward**.

**Out of bondage → Into abundance**

God does not merely break Pharaoh's hold.

He brings the faculties toward an entirely different condition of life.

The Exodus is therefore not complete when Egypt is escaped.

It has a destination.

### v10 — Moses Is Sent

> “Come now therefore, and I will send thee unto Pharaoh, that thou mayest bring forth my people the children of Israel out of Egypt.”

The man who was himself **drawn out** is now commissioned to **bring forth** the whole people.

What happened first in Moses is about to happen through Moses.

**The individual pattern becomes the collective pattern.**

### v11–12 — “Who Am I?”

And Moses said unto God:

> “Who am I, that I should go unto Pharaoh?”

This is almost the inverse of Exodus 2.

There Moses acted before being sent.

Now, when God actually sends him, Moses asks:

> **Who am I?**

And God does not answer by telling Moses who Moses is.

He says:

> **“Certainly I will be with thee.”**

That is the answer.

Moses' adequacy is not located in Moses.

The delivering consciousness becomes effective through **the Presence accompanying it**.

### v13–14 — “I AM THAT I AM”

And Moses said unto God:

> “Behold, when I come unto the children of Israel... and they shall say to me, What is his name? what shall I say unto them?”

And God said unto Moses:

> **“I AM THAT I AM.”**

And He said:

> “Thus shalt thou say unto the children of Israel, **I AM hath sent me unto you**.”

At the center of the commission is not Moses' identity but **God's identity**.

Moses asks:

**Who am I?**

The chapter answers:

**I AM.**

The delivering consciousness no longer acts from the isolated claim of personal power.

Its authority rests in **the living Presence of God**.

### v15–16 — The God of the Fathers

And God said:

> “The LORD God of your fathers, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob... this is my name for ever.”

Moses is to gather the elders of **Israel — God-Prevails** and tell them:

> “I have surely visited you, and seen that which is done to you in Egypt.”

The enslaved faculties are being called to remember that **Egypt is not their deepest identity**.

Their story did not begin with Pharaoh.

They belong to the covenant that preceded their bondage.

### v17–18 — The Call Into the Wilderness

God promises:

> “I will bring you up out of the affliction of Egypt... unto a land flowing with milk and honey.”

And Moses and the elders are to say unto Pharaoh:

> “The LORD God of the Hebrews hath met with us: and now let us go... three days' journey into the wilderness, that we may sacrifice to the LORD our God.”

The first demand upon Pharaoh is a demand for **separation for worship**.

The faculties enslaved to the foreign ruler must be released so that they may become **oriented toward God**.

Deliverance and worship are therefore joined from the beginning.

### v19–20 — Pharaoh Will Not Release Them Easily

And God said:

> “I am sure that the king of Egypt will not let you go.”

The ruling power of bondage does not voluntarily surrender the faculties it has learned to command.

Therefore:

> “I will stretch out my hand, and smite Egypt with all my wonders.”

Again the acting power is identified:

**I will stretch out MY hand.**

Moses once stretched out his own hand against the Egyptian.

Now the hand that will break Egypt is **God's**.

The deliverer has moved from **self-directed force** to participation in **divine action**.

### v21–22 — The Wealth of Egypt Comes Out Too

And God says that when Israel leaves:

> “Ye shall not go empty.”

They shall receive silver, gold, and raiment from the Egyptians.

The faculties do not merely escape the old condition.

They carry out of it **what can be reclaimed and eventually devoted to God**.

The resources once held within Egypt will later appear in the construction of the Tabernacle.

What belonged to the system of bondage can be **reoriented toward the dwelling of God**.

## Fractal Notes & References

Exodus 3 establishes a pattern that runs throughout Scripture: **separation from the old order is followed by encounter with God, and encounter with God produces commission**. Jacob is left alone before Peniel and emerges with a new identity; Moses leaves Egypt before encountering God at Horeb; Elijah comes to the same mountain in 1 Kings 19 and encounters the divine voice; the disciples encounter the risen Christ before being sent outward in Acts. The burning bush also introduces a major Exodus pattern: **God's Presence enters created form without consuming it**, anticipating the divine fire and glory that will eventually inhabit the Tabernacle. Most importantly, Exodus 2 and 3 form a precise progression: Moses first attempts deliverance through his own hand, then is separated and emptied of position, and finally learns that deliverance begins with **“I will come down,” “I will send thee,” and “I will be with thee.”** The disciple becomes useful to divine purpose when the center of action moves from **I will** to **God will**.

## Onomastics & Name Meanings

| Name | Textual / Linguistic Meaning | BKV Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Moses — Mosheh** | Exodus 2:10 connects the name with *mashah*, **“to draw out”** | **The Drawn-Out One / delivering consciousness**. Having embodied deliverance himself, Moses can now become its instrument for others. |
| **Midian — Midyan** | Commonly associated with **strife/contention**; exact etymology should remain cautious | **Separation and preparation** between bondage and divine commission |
| **Horeb — Chorev** | Commonly associated with **dryness/desolation** | **The stripped or emptied place where divine Presence is encountered** |
| **Israel — Yisrael** | Genesis 32:28 connects the name with striving with God and prevailing | **God-Prevails**; the faculties whose proper identity lies under divine rather than Egyptian government |
| **Abraham — Avraham** | Genesis 17:5 associates the name with **father of many nations** | The originating covenantal promise to which the enslaved faculties still belong |
| **Isaac — Yitschaq** | Genesis 21 connects the name with **laughter** | The promised life produced by God rather than human control |
| **Jacob — Yaʿaqov** | Genesis associates the name with the **heel / supplanting** | The grasping identity that is eventually transformed into Israel |
| **YHWH** | The divine covenant name, related in Exodus 3 to the Hebrew verb **to be** | **The self-existent and present God** from whom the authority for deliverance proceeds |
| **Ehyeh — “I AM / I WILL BE”** | First-person form of the Hebrew verb *hayah*, **to be** | The divine Presence that answers Moses' “Who am I?” with **“I will be with thee”** |

The naming dialogue in this chapter is unusually important.

Moses asks two identity questions:

**“Who am I?”**

and

**“What is His name?”**

God's response redirects the entire problem of identity away from Moses' independent ability and toward divine Presence:

**“I will be with thee.”**

**“I AM THAT I AM.”**

Moses has already learned that he is **Drawn Out**.

Now he learns **who drew him out — and whose Presence will accomplish the greater Exodus.**
