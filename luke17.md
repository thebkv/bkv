# Luke 17 — The Kingdom Is Within You

## BKV Insight

Luke 17 brings several teachings together that can seem unrelated at first. Jesus speaks about offenses, forgiveness, faith, service, cleansing, the coming of the Kingdom, Noah, Lot, and the person who tries to save his own life.

Then, near the center of the chapter, Jesus tells us where to look:

> “Neither shall they say, Lo here! or, lo there! for, behold, **the kingdom of God is within you.**”

The Pharisees ask **when** the Kingdom of God will come. Jesus answers by challenging the way they are looking for it. It does not come “with observation.” People will not be able to point and say, “Here it is,” or “There it is.”

Then comes the statement:

**the Kingdom of God is within you.**

This gives the rest of the chapter a remarkable unity.

If God's Kingdom is within, then the disciple's first concern is not finding the right external place, event, sign, or circumstance. The question becomes whether the life is actually coming under God's rule.

That is why the chapter begins with things that seem very ordinary: do not cause another person to stumble. Forgive. Exercise the faith you have. Serve without making God your debtor. Give thanks when you are healed.

Then Jesus says plainly where the Kingdom is.

And after saying it, He warns His disciples not to run after people who say:

> “See here; or, see there.”

The direction is consistent.

**Do not spend your life looking “here” and “there” for what Jesus has told you is within.**

---

## Canonical History & Context

Luke 17 takes place during Jesus' journey toward Jerusalem.

The chapter begins with Jesus speaking to His disciples. He warns them about causing others to stumble and tells them to forgive a brother who repents, even repeatedly.

The apostles respond:

> “Increase our faith.”

Jesus answers with the mustard seed and then gives the example of a servant who simply does what his master commands.

As Jesus continues toward Jerusalem, He passes through the region between Samaria and Galilee. Ten lepers call to Him for mercy. Jesus tells them to show themselves to the priests, and while they are going, they are cleansed.

Only one returns.

He is a Samaritan.

Then the Pharisees ask Jesus when the Kingdom of God will come.

Jesus tells them:

> “The kingdom of God cometh not with observation.”

And:

> “the kingdom of God is within you.”

He then turns specifically to His disciples and speaks about the days of the Son of Man. He compares them with the days of Noah and Lot, when ordinary life continued right up to the moment of sudden judgment.

His warning becomes very simple:

Do not turn back.

Remember Lot's wife.

Do not try to preserve the life that must be surrendered.

The chapter closes with a difficult saying about two people together, one taken and the other left, followed by the disciples asking:

> “Where, Lord?”

Jesus answers:

> “Wheresoever the body is, thither will the eagles be gathered together.”

That final section should be read carefully in the setting Jesus Himself has just given it: Noah, Lot, judgment, separation, and the danger of trying to preserve the old life.

## Natural Reading

### v1–4 — Watch Yourselves

Jesus begins:

> “It is impossible but that offences will come.”

The Greek word behind **offences**, *skandala*, refers to stumbling blocks or traps—things that cause someone to fall.

Jesus does not tell His disciples that they can create a world in which no stumbling blocks ever appear.

Instead He warns them:

> “but woe unto him, through whom they come!”

Then He says:

> **“Take heed to yourselves.”**

The disciple begins with his own conduct.

Do not become the thing that causes another person to fall.

Then Jesus turns immediately to forgiveness.

If your brother sins against you, rebuke him. If he repents, forgive him.

And if he returns seven times in one day saying:

> “I repent”

Jesus says:

> **“thou shalt forgive him.”**

This is not passive indifference to wrongdoing. Jesus includes both **rebuke** and **forgiveness**.

Something happened.

It can be named.

But when repentance comes, the disciple does not keep the offense alive so that it can continue ruling the relationship.

For BKV, this is already Kingdom government in practice.

If God's rule is becoming active within us, then resentment cannot simply be allowed to occupy the throne.

---

### v5–6 — “Increase Our Faith”

The apostles respond:

> **“Increase our faith.”**

It sounds like the obvious request.

Give us more.

Jesus answers:

> “If ye had faith as a grain of mustard seed, ye might say unto this sycamine tree, Be thou plucked up by the root, and be thou planted in the sea; and it should obey you.”

Jesus does not answer by giving them a measurement of how much faith they need.

He points instead to something extremely small.

A mustard seed.

The picture shifts the emphasis away from sheer quantity. Faith does not need to look enormous before it can act.

The disciple can easily spend years thinking:

**When I have enough faith, then something will happen.**

Jesus points to a seed.

Use what is alive.

---

### v7–10 — The Servant Does What His Master Says

Jesus now gives an example from ordinary household life.

A servant has been plowing or feeding cattle. When he comes in from the field, the master does not immediately become the servant and begin waiting on him.

The servant continues doing what belongs to his service.

Jesus concludes:

> “So likewise ye, when ye shall have done all those things which are commanded you, say, We are unprofitable servants: we have done that which was our duty to do.”

The point is not that obedience has no value.

The point is that obedience does not place God in our debt.

The disciple does not obey God and then say:

**Now God owes me the result I want.**

The servant serves because the master is the master.

This fits naturally with the Kingdom teaching that will come later in the chapter. A kingdom has a king. If God rules, obedience is not a bargain made with Him.

It is what follows from His rule.

---

### v11–14 — Ten Lepers Cry for Mercy

Jesus continues toward Jerusalem and passes between Samaria and Galilee.

Ten men with leprosy stand at a distance and cry:

> **“Jesus, Master, have mercy on us.”**

Jesus tells them:

> “Go shew yourselves unto the priests.”

Notice what happens next:

> **“as they went, they were cleansed.”**

They have to begin moving before they see the cleansing.

Jesus gives the instruction.

They go.

While they are going, the condition changes.

This is one of the chapter's clearest pictures of faith as action. The ten men do not stand still waiting for visible proof before obeying.

**They move according to the word they have been given.**

Then the cleansing appears.

---

### v15–19 — One Comes Back

One of the ten sees that he has been healed.

He turns back.

He glorifies God with a loud voice, falls at Jesus' feet, and gives thanks.

Luke then adds:

> **“and he was a Samaritan.”**

Jesus asks:

> “Were there not ten cleansed? but where are the nine?”

All ten received cleansing.

One returned to the source.

That gives us an important distinction without having to force one onto the story.

**Receiving something from God and recognizing God in what has happened are not quite the same thing.**

The Samaritan does more than enjoy his changed condition.

He returns.

He gives glory to God.

He gives thanks.

And Jesus tells him:

> “Arise, go thy way: thy faith hath made thee whole.”

The life under God's rule is not merely a life that receives.

It becomes a life that recognizes, returns, and gives thanks.

---

### v20–21 — Where Is the Kingdom?

Now the Pharisees ask Jesus:

> **“when the kingdom of God should come”**

The question is about **when**.

Jesus answers:

> **“The kingdom of God cometh not with observation.”**

The word translated **observation** is *paratērēsis*. It carries the idea of watching or observing closely.

Then Jesus continues:

> “Neither shall they say, Lo here! or, lo there!”

Now the question has moved from **when** to **where**.

Not here.

Not there.

Then Jesus says:

> **“for, behold, the kingdom of God is within you.”**

The Greek is:

**ἡ βασιλεία τοῦ Θεοῦ ἐντὸς ὑμῶν ἐστιν**

*Hē basileia tou Theou entos hymōn estin.*

At the center is:

**ἐντὸς ὑμῶν — entos hymōn**

**entos** means **inside, within, on the inside**. The phrase can be debated in this verse because the pronoun is plural, and some translations therefore render it “among you” or “in your midst.” The KJV renders the phrase directly:

> **“within you.”**

The underlying Greek genuinely permits the interior reading; *entos* itself is an inward word. The same word appears in Matthew 23:26 when Jesus speaks of cleansing **“the inside”** of the cup and platter.  [oai_citation:0‡Bible Hub](https://biblehub.com/text/luke/17-21.htm?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

That matters especially because Luke does not leave the statement alone.

Jesus has just rejected:

**here**

and:

**there.**

Then He tells His disciples in the very next section not to chase reports that the Son of Man can be found:

**here**

or:

**there.**

The movement of the teaching is therefore difficult to miss.

The Pharisees are looking for an observable Kingdom that can be located externally.

Jesus redirects the search.

For BKV, this is one of the clearest statements in Scripture:

**the Kingdom is the rule of God within the person.**

It is not merely a place we hope to reach.

It is not merely a political order we expect someone else to establish.

It is not something we can locate by pointing across the landscape.

The King establishes His government within.

And from there, the life changes outward.

---

### v22–25 — Do Not Go After Them

Jesus now turns from the Pharisees to His disciples.

He tells them that days will come when they will desire to see one of the days of the Son of Man.

Then comes another warning:

> “And they shall say to you, See here; or, see there: go not after them, nor follow them.”

The language should sound familiar.

Only moments earlier:

> “Lo here! or, lo there!”

Now:

> “See here; or, see there.”

And Jesus says:

> **“go not after them.”**

This strengthens the direction already given in vv.20–21.

Do not spend your life running from place to place because someone claims that the decisive appearance of God's Kingdom is over there.

Jesus compares the Son of Man to lightning:

> “that lighteneth out of the one part under heaven, shineth unto the other part under heaven”

It does not need someone standing beside it saying:

**Look over there.**

When it is revealed, it is unmistakable.

But before that:

> “first must he suffer many things, and be rejected of this generation.”

Jesus does not separate glory from the path that leads through suffering.

---

### v26–30 — Noah and Lot

Jesus now reaches back into Scripture.

First Noah.

People were:

> “eating, they drank, they married wives, they were given in marriage”

right up until Noah entered the ark.

Then the flood came.

Next Lot.

People:

> “did eat, they drank, they bought, they sold, they planted, they builded”

right up until Lot left Sodom.

Then judgment came.

Notice that most of the activities Jesus lists are not evil in themselves.

Eating.

Drinking.

Marriage.

Buying.

Selling.

Planting.

Building.

The problem is not ordinary life.

The problem is **being completely absorbed in the visible order while remaining unaware of what God is doing.**

Everything looks normal.

Until suddenly it is not.

For the disciple, this is a serious warning. The outward world can continue looking completely ordinary while a decisive change is already underway.

---

### v31–32 — Remember Lot's Wife

Jesus makes the warning personal.

If someone is on the housetop when the moment comes, he must not go back down for his possessions.

If someone is in the field:

> “let him likewise not return back.”

Then Jesus gives one of the shortest commands in the chapter:

> **“Remember Lot's wife.”**

She had left Sodom.

But she looked back.

The body had moved away from the old place while something in her was still turned toward it.

For the disciple, the warning is immediate.

**When God is bringing you out, do not keep turning inwardly toward the thing He is bringing you out of.**

Exodus gives us the same problem on a larger scale.

Israel can leave Egypt while still wanting Egypt.

Physical departure is not always complete deliverance.

The old government can survive in desire long after the old place has been left behind.

Jesus says:

**Remember Lot's wife.**

---

### v33 — Losing the Life You Tried to Save

Jesus now gives the principle underneath the warning:

> **“Whosoever shall seek to save his life shall lose it; and whosoever shall lose his life shall preserve it.”**

The natural impulse is preservation.

Hold onto what I have.

Protect the identity I know.

Keep control.

Make sure nothing is lost.

Jesus says that this very attempt can become the cause of loss.

The disciple therefore faces a strange but recurring law of the Kingdom:

**the life we desperately try to preserve cannot become the life God is giving us.**

Something has to be surrendered.

Lot's wife tries to keep a relationship with the world she has been told to leave.

Jesus tells the disciple not to do the same.

---

### v34–36 — One Taken, the Other Left

Jesus then gives three closely related pictures:

Two people in one bed.

Two women grinding together.

Two people in the field.

One is taken.

The other is left.

These verses have accumulated a great deal of later interpretive machinery, but Luke gives us an immediate context before we add anything else.

Jesus has just spoken about **Noah** and **Lot**.

In both stories, people who appear to be living together under the same ordinary conditions suddenly experience a separation.

So the minimum sufficient reading is clear:

**a division occurs where moments before everything appeared joined together.**

For BKV, that pattern can also be read inwardly without pretending that the historical warning disappears.

The Kingdom brings separation within the disciple.

Not everything in the old life can enter the new order unchanged.

Some things belong to the Kingdom.

Some things belong to the order being left behind.

The disciple cannot preserve both forever.

---

### v37 — “Where, Lord?”

The disciples ask:

> **“Where, Lord?”**

It is an interesting question after everything Jesus has just said.

The Pharisees asked **when** the Kingdom would come.

Jesus rejected **here** and **there**.

The disciples now ask:

**Where?**

Jesus answers:

> “Wheresoever the body is, thither will the eagles be gathered together.”

The saying is difficult, and we should not pretend that its exact force is simpler than it is.

The word translated **eagles**, *aetoi*, can refer to eagles, though the image in this saying is often understood as carrion birds gathering around a corpse.

At minimum, Jesus gives them an image in which the condition itself reveals where the gathering will occur.

The sign follows the reality.

You do not need someone to point and announce:

**There it is.**

What is present eventually becomes visible by what gathers around it.

That fits the larger warning of the chapter remarkably well:

**the hidden condition comes first; the visible result follows.**

## Fractal Notes & References

Luke 17 becomes much more coherent when its concrete scenes are allowed to accumulate before we name the pattern.

A brother sins against you.

What rules you—resentment or forgiveness?

The apostles want more faith.

Jesus points to a seed.

A servant finishes his work.

Does obedience make the master his debtor?

Ten lepers are cleansed.

Who recognizes the source and returns?

The Pharisees look for the Kingdom.

Where are they looking?

Outside.

Jesus says:

> **“the kingdom of God is within you.”**

Then He immediately warns His disciples not to run after people saying:

> “See here; or, see there.”

Then Noah and Lot appear.

People remain occupied with the visible world while something much greater is happening.

Then Lot's wife looks back.

Then Jesus says that the person who tries to preserve his life will lose it.

Then comes separation.

**The whole chapter keeps moving from the visible condition toward the hidden government underneath it.**

This is why Luke 17:20–21 belongs near the center of the BKV framework.

The Kingdom is not merely another object inside the disciple.

It is **government**.

The Greek **basileia** can speak of kingship, royal rule, reign, or kingdom. So when Jesus speaks of the *basileia tou Theou*, we should hear more than geography.

**God reigns.**

And if that reign is within, then the great question of discipleship becomes:

**What is ruling here?**

That question reaches backward into Exodus.

Pharaoh rules Israel's powers for his own purposes.

God brings Israel out.

The wilderness exposes how much of Pharaoh's government remains within the people even after Pharaoh himself has been left behind.

Then Scripture repeatedly brings the same question back at different scales:

**Who rules?**

Luke 17 gives Jesus' answer a location:

> **“the kingdom of God is within you.”**

The disciple therefore does not merely wait for God's Kingdom.

He learns to live from it.

### The Central Phrase

**ἡ βασιλεία τοῦ Θεοῦ ἐντὸς ὑμῶν ἐστιν**

**The Kingdom of God is within you.**

The KJV preserves the inward force of **entos** directly. Other translations choose “among you” or “in your midst,” showing that the construction has an interpretive question at the phrase level. But *entos* itself means **inside/within**, and its other New Testament occurrence refers plainly to the inside of the cup in Matthew 23:26.  [oai_citation:2‡Bible Hub](https://biblehub.com/text/luke/17-21.htm?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

For BKV, we should therefore neither hide the translation question nor allow it to erase what Jesus actually says.

The chapter begins with the disciple watching what happens **within his own conduct**.

It moves through faith, obedience, cleansing, recognition, and gratitude.

Then Jesus rejects the search for the Kingdom:

**here**

or:

**there**

and says:

**within you.**

Afterward He again warns:

**do not run after “here” and “there.”**

Then comes the final demand:

Do not look back.

Do not try to preserve the old life.

Let the separation occur.

Because the Kingdom Jesus is describing is not merely something the disciple will someday observe.

**It is the rule of God becoming active within him now.**
