# ACTS 20

## 20 — THE LAST FAREWELL

Paul is nearing the end of this part of his journey, and the chapter shows what it means to remain faithful when the road ahead becomes difficult. At Troas, a young man named Eutychus falls asleep while Paul is speaking, falls from a third-story window, and is taken up dead. Paul goes down to him, embraces him, and his life is restored. The disciple should recognize the warning here. It is possible to be in the company of believers, hearing the Word, and yet fall asleep to what is happening within. But the same power that raised the young man is able to restore the life that has fallen.

Paul then calls the elders of Ephesus to meet him at Miletus. He knows that suffering awaits him in Jerusalem, but he refuses to abandon the work Jesus has given him. He reminds them that he has taught them everything profitable, held nothing back, and served without seeking their possessions. Now they must watch over themselves and the people entrusted to them. Paul warns that some will come from outside to destroy the flock, while others will arise from within and draw disciples after themselves. The disciple must learn this distinction. Not every spiritual danger comes from an obvious enemy. Sometimes the desire for followers, authority, or recognition begins within the very people who have been entrusted with the work.

Paul's farewell gives us a picture of spiritual maturity. He has spent himself in service, and now he must leave those he loves in God's hands. He kneels with them, prays, and they weep together before accompanying him to the ship. His work among them has been completed, but the life of God that brought them together will continue.

## The Savior Test

Paul recalls the words of Jesus:

> "It is more blessed to give than to receive." — Acts 20:35

This is the key to Paul's entire farewell. Jesus taught that greatness is found in service, and Paul has lived that teaching before the disciples. He has given them his labor, his teaching, his prayers, and his love without seeking their wealth or allegiance to himself.

Jesus also warned his disciples to watch for false prophets who come in sheep's clothing (Matthew 7:15). Paul's warning about wolves entering the flock confirms the same principle. The disciple must examine both the teaching he receives and the motives of his own heart.

The chapter asks a serious question: Are you seeking to gather something for yourself, or are you giving what God has placed in your hands? Paul shows the disciple a life that has become free enough to serve, faithful enough to finish its course, and loving enough to let others continue without him.
